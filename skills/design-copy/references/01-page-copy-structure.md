# Page copy structure: hero, sections, CTAs, and copy in layout

Persuasion strategy (awareness levels, big idea, leads, offers) lives in the `copywriting` skill. Positioning and ICP live in `website-strategy`. This chapter covers what each block of a page must say, in what order, at what length, so the words and the layout work as one.

## Principles
1. **Clarity first, then persuasion.** A visitor decides in about 10 seconds whether to stay (NN/g 2011). A page that is not understood cannot persuade. Concise, scannable, objective text together improved usability 124% in NN/g's 1997 test.
2. **Every block answers one visitor question, in order.** Wynter's layers: What is it? Is it for me? Do I want it? Why you? (and, under every layer: why should I believe you, what could go wrong). If a layer fails, everything after it fails too.
3. **Specific beats impressive.** Harry Dry's three tests (practitioner): can I visualize it, can I falsify it, can nobody else say it. "Never write an ad your competitor can sign" (Jim Durfee).
4. **Copy and layout are one design.** Each slot has a job and a word budget. When they fight, cut words before shrinking type.

## Fundamentals (gate)
- Hero headline or subheadline says what the product is and who it is for, in the visitor's words. Test: if the visitor reads only this, do they know what you sell? (Julian Shapiro, practitioner; NN/g homepage principle 2).
- No "Welcome to X", no slogans with no noun ("Supercharge your workflow"), no "Powered by AI" as the value (NN/g 2025).
- Every CTA label names the outcome. Banned as primary labels: Learn more, Click here, Submit, Get started (when the product is unclear), Explore (NN/g 2024).
- Sentence case for every headline and button (Microsoft, GOV.UK).
- Real copy in every layout review. Lorem ipsum hides length and hierarchy failures.

## Decision rules

### Hero
- **Headline (H1):** the outcome or the category plus differentiator. 4 to 10 words (opinion, from the slot budget below). If the headline is clever, the subheadline must pass the "only this text" test alone.
- **Subheadline:** 1 to 2 sentences, 15 to 30 words. Sentence 1: what it is, for whom. Sentence 2: how the headline is possible, or the biggest objection answered (Shapiro).
- **Primary CTA:** verb + object, 2 to 4 words, continues the headline's promise ("Start matching invoices", not "Sign up").
- **Risk reducer under the CTA:** one line, 3 to 8 words, answering the top friction: "Free for 14 days. No card needed." / "Cancel anytime."
- **Proof right under the hero:** logos, a number, or one named quote. Social proof comes before the first long feature section (Shapiro).
- If the name does not say what you do, the tagline must (NN/g homepage principle 2).

### Choosing the headline type
| Situation | Headline type | Example shape |
|---|---|---|
| New category, visitor does not know it exists | Category + outcome | "Invoice matching that closes the books in a day" |
| Crowded category, visitor knows options | Differentiator or objection | "Design subscriptions for everyone" + "Pause or cancel anytime." (Designjoy) |
| Known brand, returning visitors | Task or newest value | "Plan your next trip" |
| One-offer landing page from an ad | Repeat the ad's promise nearly word for word (message match; practitioner consensus in CRO, no single study cited) | Ad: "Payroll in 5 minutes" / H1: "Run payroll in 5 minutes" |

For awareness-level selection beyond this table, use `copywriting` (Schwartz).

**Map the whole scent chain before judging a headline (lesson, Stand Firm quiz cover, 2026-10-28).** On a funnel page the visitor arrives carrying words: the video's spoken ask, the caption, the bio, the DM, the share card. Write that chain down from the client's own files first. If one phrase runs through every link (here "What kind of strong are you?"), the H1 repeats it, and new headlines compete only as challengers in a later split test. Any headline test, simulated or real, must show readers the inbound chain; a test that shows the page cold will score the echo as "generic" and pick the wrong line (it did). Opinion from that job, consistent with the message match row above.

### Section sequences
**SaaS or product homepage (default):** nav, hero (headline, sub, CTA, risk reducer), proof strip, problem or "before" (optional, 1 short block), 3 to 6 feature or benefit blocks each tied to one objection, how it works (3 steps), deeper proof (case study numbers, testimonials with name and role), pricing teaser or plans, FAQ (top 5 to 8 objections), final CTA repeating the hero promise, footer.

**Single-offer landing page:** no main nav; hero; proof; benefits; mechanism or how it works; offer details; FAQ; final CTA. One goal, one CTA verb repeated.

**Service or studio site:** hero (what you make, for whom); selected work with outcomes; services as deliverables; process in 3 to 5 steps; proof; pricing or "starting at"; CTA to a specific next step (book a 15 minute call, request a quote).

### Feature blocks
- Header: a short benefit statement with a concrete noun or number, not a label. "Matches 9 in 10 invoices automatically", not "Smart matching".
- Body: 1 to 3 sentences, max 40 words. Says what it does, then kills the matching objection.
- Each block maps to one objection from customer research ("What almost stopped you from buying?", Shapiro). If no objection, question the block.

### CTA ladder
| Commitment | Label pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| High (buy, start) | Verb + outcome | "Start free trial", "Book my call" |
| Medium (try, see) | Verb + thing they get | "See pricing", "Watch 2 minute demo" |
| Low (read) | Specific destination | "Read the Acme case study" |
- One primary CTA style per view; the secondary is visibly quieter (hand-off: design-layout).
- Same verb for the same action everywhere on the site.
- First-person ("Start my trial") vs second-person: one widely cited test showed a large lift (Aagaard, ContentVerve); it is unreplicated. Treat as a test, not a rule. Whichever you use, keep one perspective on the page; "Start my subscription" next to "Your first bag ships" switches twice (Apple HIG, Writing, 2025: "try not to switch perspectives").

### Links
- 4 Ss: specific, sincere, substantial, succinct (NN/g). The first 2 words carry it (NN/g first 2 words study: 35% of links unpredictable from first 11 characters).
- Never "Learn more" alone. If the design system demands short link text, the accessible name must still be unique (WCAG 2.4.4) and contain the visible text (2.5.3): visible "Learn more", accessible name "Learn more about pricing".

## Numbers: slot budgets
Derived from the shared type scale (desktop display sizes) and measure (45 to 75 characters, 66 ideal). Opinion where noted.

| Slot | Words | Characters | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero H1 | 4 to 10 | 20 to 60 | 2 lines max at desktop display size, 3 on 375px phone |
| Hero subheadline | 15 to 30 | 90 to 180 | Keep to the 45 to 75 character measure per line |
| Primary button | 2 to 4 | 10 to 25 | Leave room for 100%+ translation growth (see reference 05) |
| Risk reducer | 3 to 8 | under 50 | Smaller text, still 4.5:1 contrast |
| Feature header | 3 to 8 | under 50 | |
| Feature body | 15 to 40 | under 250 | |
| Testimonial | 15 to 40 | | Name, role, company, photo; specific result beats praise |
| FAQ answer | 20 to 80 | | First sentence answers yes or no |
Budgets are opinion calibrated to NN/g reading data (users read 20 to 28% of words; half a page is read only under 111 words).

## Do and don't
| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| "Empowering teams to do their best work" | "Shared to-do lists for teams of 5 to 50" |
| "Welcome to Brightline" | "Bookkeeping for Ontario restaurants, done in 48 hours" |
| "Learn more" | "See how matching works" |
| "Smart automation" | "Matches 9 in 10 invoices without you" (only if true and provable) |
| "Trusted by thousands" | "Used by 4,200 accounting firms" |
| "Get started" on a page that never said what it is | Fix the hero first; then "Create free account" |

## Worked examples
**1. B2B SaaS, invoice matching for accounting firms.** Layer check: clarity fails if the H1 says "Close faster". Draft: H1 "Match every invoice to every payment, automatically." Sub: "Ledgerline reads your bank feeds and invoices and pairs them for you. Firms using it close month-end 4 days sooner." (the number must be real; if no data, use the mechanism: "...and flags only the ones it is not sure about"). CTA "Start matching free". Risk reducer "14 days free. Works with Xero and QuickBooks." The integration line kills the biggest objection (will it work with our stack?).

**2. Rewrite "Empowering teams to do their best work."** Fails all three Dry tests. Ask: what does it do, for whom, what is the before? Product is async video updates for remote teams. Rewrite: H1 "Replace your daily standup with 2 minute videos." Sub: "Your team records a quick update when it suits them. Everyone catches up in 5 minutes, in any time zone." CTA "Try it with your team".

**3. Designjoy (practitioner case).** "Design subscriptions for everyone" names the category; "Pause or cancel anytime." kills the subscription objection in four words. An earlier "design agency with a twist" was criticized by readers as unclear. Lesson: name the category plainly and spend the second line on the top objection.
