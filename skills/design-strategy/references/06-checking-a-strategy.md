# 06 Checking a strategy

> design-strategy reference. Loaded by design-director after design-foundations and before the playbook; read the file the SKILL.md map names. Builds on website-strategy and crafting-voice-identity (Davon's skills) and never restates them.

Research notes: `kb/research/strategy/06-checking-a-strategy.md` (F-numbers). Opinion is marked (opinion).

A strategy is checked three ways: is it **distinctive** (it could not be a competitor's), is it **clear** (a cold viewer gets it in five seconds), and is it **true** (every claim traces to a fact held as evidence). Each check below runs with what a single builder has: screenshots, a fresh-context subagent, the claims inventory. Real people beat all of them; say which you used.

## Floor: fundamentals (non-negotiable)
1. **Your own judgment never counts as a cold viewer.** You know the idea. A fresh-context subagent shown only the image counts as one viewer (design-playbook-brand 2a) [F2]. [judged]
2. **Every public claim has a ledger row**: claim, fact, source with its date, evidence held (yes or cut). Numbers and dates also pass `claims_inventory.mjs` (FOUNDATIONS row 16) [F7]. [lint: `claims_inventory.mjs` on the page; `brief_lint.mjs` checks every ledger row has all four cells and "yes" or "cut"]
3. **The swap tests are run and recorded**: words (onlyness and hero) and first screen (hide-the-logo). [lint: `brief_lint.mjs` checks a result is written; judged for honesty]
4. **Results go in the hand-over**, including fails and what changed. [judged]
5. **Identity is tested by recognition, not liking** (05). [judged]

## Principles
1. **Test the three layers separately.** Words (does the hero say something only we can say?), look (is it ours without the logo?), truth (can we prove it?). One pass does not cover the others (opinion).
2. **Find problems, do not estimate shares.** Five viewers or 12 to 13 message-test responses find what is unclear [F3]; they do not measure fame. Fame and uniqueness need a survey of category buyers (05).
3. **Put the competitor next to you, at the same size.** Distinctiveness is relative; a page reviewed alone always looks distinctive (opinion).

## Decision rules
### Which checks, by depth
| Depth | Run |
|---|---|
| Inherit | Swap test on the new screen's headline; claims ledger for any new number |
| Three lines | Competitor wall (first screens or posters), hide-the-logo, five-second test with 1 or more fresh viewers, claims ledger |
| One page | All of the above, plus the onlyness swap test on the brief and a fresh-context critic on the hero line |
| Full (new brand, rebrand) | All of the above, plus the mark wall (design-graphic 01), the asset audit (05), and a real message test or recognition test when budget allows |
| After launch (any brand with an identity table) | Check 9: re-measure fame and uniqueness on the 05 cadence |

### The checks
1. **Word swap test** (03): put each named competitor's name into the onlyness sentence and the hero line. Pass: false for all three.
2. **Competitor wall**: screenshot the first screen of the three named competitors at 1440x900 and 390x844 and ours, same size, side by side, logos visible. Then a second row with all logos covered. Pass (opinion): a fresh viewer can pick ours out of the covered row and say what makes it different in one phrase.
3. **Hide-the-logo** (design-director step 11) [F5]: cover the logo and name on our page. Could the visuals belong to a competitor? If yes, art direction failed.
4. **Mark wall** (full depth): our mark among 12 to 20 category marks, grayscale at 48px [F6].
5. **Five-second test** [F1]: show the first screen for 5 seconds to a cold viewer, hide it, ask: "What is this?", "Who is it for?", "What would you do next?", and for brand pieces "Is anything wrong with it?" (design-playbook-brand 2a). Pass: category, audience and action named correctly; nothing read as an error.
6. **Message test** (when the page will carry spend or a launch): show the hero and key sections to the target audience; score clarity, relevance, value and differentiation [F3]. About 12 to 13 responses reach saturation in Wynter's guidance [F3]. A fresh-context subagent briefed with the audience moment is a stand-in, labelled as such (opinion).
7. **Claims ledger**: one row per public claim. Claim as written; the fact; the source (document, data, test, date); evidence held: yes, or cut. A claim without evidence is rewritten as the fact it rests on, or cut (03; FTC, CAP 3.7).
8. **Recognition test** (identity, full depth). An existing asset: shown without the name to category buyers, counting correct links and competitor links (the 05 audit). A new asset has no fame before launch, so test two things instead (opinion on the method): **misattribution**, shown cold and unnamed, does any viewer name a competitor or the category leader (any competitor link is a uniqueness warning); and **learnability**, shown once in context with the name, then unnamed among the covered competitor wall, is it picked as this brand? A fresh-context subagent can run both; only category buyers can measure fame.
9. **Tracking** (after launch; 05 "After launch"). Same assets, same question, same kind of sample (category buyers), about one purchase cycle after launch (3 to 12 months), then yearly and before any redesign (opinion on the cadence). Record fame, uniqueness and quadrant per asset next to the previous round. A change of sample or wording breaks the comparison; note it.
10. **Behavior check** (one page and full): for each brand behavior in the brief (05), ask whether a named competitor could copy it next week at no cost. Yes means it is not proof; find another or say so in the weakest field (opinion).

### Reading the results
- **One viewer misreads the category**: the category word is missing or below the first screen (design-playbook-landing non-negotiable 2). Fix the hero, not the viewer.
- **The covered wall cannot tell us apart**: the expressive code break (05) is too weak or absent. Strengthen the one break; do not add three.
- **Message test: high clarity, low differentiation**: the words are fine and generic; go back to 03 step 2 (unique attributes), not to copy polish.
- **Uniqueness falls between tracking rounds while fame holds**: a competitor is using a similar asset. Strengthen co-presentation with the name and the asset's most ownable detail before fame erodes too (05) (opinion).
- **A claim has no evidence**: rewrite as the underlying fact or cut it. Never soften "only" to "one of the only"; it is still a claim (opinion).

## Numbers
| Item | Default |
|---|---|
| Five-second exposure | 5 s [F1] |
| Cold viewers per test | 3 to 5 (opinion, design-playbook-brand 2a); at least 1 fresh-context subagent when no people |
| Message test responses | about 12 to 13 to saturation [F3] |
| Mark wall | 12 to 20 marks, 48px grayscale (opinion) [F6] |
| Competitors on the screen wall | 3, at 1440x900 and 390x844 (opinion) |
| Tracking cadence | about one purchase cycle after launch (3 to 12 months), then yearly, and before any redesign (opinion; 05) |

## Ceiling: expert moves
1. **Cover the logos and ask the viewer to pick yours.** A distinctiveness check with a correct answer is harder to fool than "does it look different?" (opinion; it mirrors the asset audit's recognition question [05]).
2. **Brief the fresh viewer with the audience moment, not the strategy.** Tell the subagent who it is and what situation it is in (02); never the positioning or concept. Otherwise it confirms the strategy instead of testing it (opinion).
3. **Diagnose by dimension.** Clarity fails are copy fixes; relevance fails are audience (02) fixes; differentiation fails are positioning (03) fixes. Fixing the wrong layer is the most common waste after a message test (opinion, from Wynter's four dimensions [F3]).
4. **Ledger the visual claims too.** A photo of a "customer" who is a model, a chart that implies growth, a "Featured in" logo strip: each is a claim and needs a row (design-art-direction fundamental 8: never present stock, AI or drawn people as real customers) (opinion on the ledger rule).
5. **Re-run the wall after the build, not only on the brief.** Strategy that passed on paper often collapses into category defaults during layout; the built first screen is the one that competes (opinion).

## Do and don't
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Fresh subagent, 5 s, named category and audience; did not name the action. Moved the button up." | "Looks clear to me" |
| Ledger row: "2 business days / payout data Q3 / ops report / yes" | "Fast payouts" with no source |
| Covered-logo wall at the same size | Our page reviewed alone |
| Recognition count with the name hidden | "The team loved it" |

## Worked examples
### 1. Invoicing tool hero (one page)
- Word swap: "Know when each client will actually pay" is false for the three named competitors. Pass.
- Wall: covered row; the fresh viewer picked ours by "the timeline of expected payments" (the product's own data as the hero image). Pass.
- Five-second test: named "invoicing", "freelancers", "start free trial". Pass.
- Ledger: "Clients pay 9 days sooner on average" had no dated study. Rewritten as the fact the product does hold: "See each client's average days late, from your own invoices." Pass.

### 2. Festival poster (three lines)
- Wall: three other festival posters (Didone titles, misregistration). Ours, a red sandstone wall of cream capitals, was picked from the covered row.
- Five-second test with a fresh agent: read the name, dates and city; nothing read as an error (the held-out cold-viewer check, `kb/tests/heldout.md`).
- Ledger: dates weekday-checked against 2027; prices matched the ticket offers.

## Where this domain is usually wrong
1. **"We reviewed it ourselves."** The builder cannot be a cold viewer; the house tests showed self-review passing what a fresh critic failed (design-director step 13) [F2].
2. **"Five users told us they liked it."** Small tests find problems; they do not measure preference or fame. Use recognition and the four message dimensions, not liking (05, [F3]).
3. **"Puffery is fine."** Obvious exaggeration is allowed; claims a consumer takes as objective need evidence held first (CAP 3.2 and 3.7; FTC) (03).
