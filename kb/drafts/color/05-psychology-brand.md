# Color psychology and brand

What the evidence says, what is convention, what is folklore, and how to choose a brand color without the folklore. Sources: `kb/research/color/05-psychology-brand.md`. Positioning work belongs to the website-strategy skill; this file covers only the color decision.

## Fundamentals (non-negotiable)
1. **Label every color claim** as one of: evidence (peer reviewed, replicated), convention (what a category or culture expects), or folklore (repeated, unsupported). Say which when you advise a client.
2. **Context decides meaning.** "All color effects undoubtedly depend on certain conditions" such as culture, task, and the exact color (Elliot 2015). Red means danger in some cultures and luck or prosperity in others; Apple's Stocks app shows rising prices in red for Chinese (Apple HIG).
3. **Brand color never overrides accessibility.** If the brand color fails as text, it changes role (fill, accent, illustration), not the law (see 02 and 03).
4. **Semantic colors outrank brand colors.** Danger, success and warning keep their conventional hues even when the brand fights them.

## What the evidence actually supports
| Claim | Strength | Use it how |
|---|---|---|
| The field is young; small-sample findings are preliminary. | Strong (Elliot 2015, leading researcher's review) | Be humble in client decks. |
| Red draws attention. | Replicated in 5+ labs, some contrary studies | Red for the one thing that must be noticed (errors, destructive actions). |
| Seeing red before a hard cognitive task can hurt performance; red in achievement settings raises caution and avoidance. | Replicated, with contrary studies | Avoid red as the dominant color on test, quiz, checkout or form screens (our inference). |
| Red raises perceived aggressiveness and dominance. | Replicated, with contrary studies | Fine for sport and energy brands; risky for care and finance (our inference). |
| Hue, saturation and value shift perceived brand personality (red tied to excitement, blue to competence) and can shift purchase intent. | One paper set, lab studies (Labrecque and Milne 2012) | A weak nudge. Category fit and execution matter more. |

## Folklore to drop
- **"62 to 90 percent of snap judgments are based on color alone."** It appears in the abstract of Singh (2006) with no citation and no data behind it. Do not repeat it.
- **Hue-emotion tables** ("blue = trust", "green = health", "orange = cheap") as causal laws. They are category conventions at best.
- **"Red buttons convert better."** Only anecdotes. Our opinion: when a button color "wins", the likely cause is that it contrasts with the rest of the page.

## Decision rules for choosing a brand hue
1. **Map the category.** List the 8 to 12 direct competitors and plot their primary hues on a wheel (use the website-strategy skill for the competitor set). This shows the convention (for example, many finance brands in blue).
2. **Choose: conform or own.** Conform when trust and familiarity are the job (regulated, high-risk purchases). Own an empty sector of the wheel when differentiation is the job (crowded, low-differentiation categories). Opinion: most small clients win by owning a hue their three nearest competitors do not use.
3. **Check semantic collisions.** A red or green brand complicates error and success states. Plan the shift (02) before committing.
4. **Pick chroma and lightness for personality, hue for ownership.** Calm, premium and serious read through low chroma and deep lightness more than through any hue (our opinion). Loud and playful read through high chroma.
5. **Test ownability in use, not on a swatch.** Mock the brand color on a button, a header and an illustration next to two competitors' screenshots. If you cannot tell them apart at a glance, keep looking.
6. **Check culture** for each market the client sells in (Apple HIG).

## How to present color to a client
- Show the palette in use on real screens, both themes, never as isolated swatches (Albers: color is relative).
- Give the reason in their terms: "this owns the green space none of your three competitors use, and it reads calm because it is low chroma", not "green means growth".
- Show the accessibility consequence of their favorite: "your yellow cannot carry white text; here is how we keep it loud and legible."
- Mark opinions as opinions. Clients trust designers who separate taste from evidence.

## Expert moves
- **The brand is a system, not a swatch.** Ownability comes from the combination: hue plus neutral temperature plus how much color appears. Two brands with the same blue feel different if one is 5% color on warm paper and the other is 40% color on cool gray (our opinion).
- **Name colors by role in the brand book and by number in the code.** The client gets "Harbor Blue"; the code gets `--color-blue-600`.
- **Use the evidence where it is strong:** red for attention and stop, used sparingly so it keeps its power.

## Do and don't
- Do: "Green is a category convention for wellness, so it signals 'you are in the right place'; we will own a teal-sage corner to stand apart." Don't: "Green means health."
- Do: cite Elliot when a client asks "does color affect behavior?". Don't: cite the 62 to 90 percent figure.

## Worked example: "What color should my client's wellness brand be? I read green means health."
1. Reframe: green does not "mean" health; it is the category convention, which helps recognition and hurts differentiation.
2. Map competitors (website-strategy skill): suppose five of eight use mid green and two use lavender.
3. Options: (a) conform: sage green, low chroma (0.06 to 0.09), warm neutrals, for a client whose job is trust; (b) own: terracotta or deep plum, for a client whose job is standing out.
4. Constraints: if green is the brand, success states shift to blue-green or use icons strongly; if terracotta, danger shifts to crimson.
5. Build the scale with the ladder (02) and show both options on the client's real hero, pricing and booking screens, light and dark.
6. Tell the client which parts are evidence (very little), convention (green in wellness), and our judgment (ownability).
