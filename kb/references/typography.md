# Typography
Where to go deeper on type: choosing and pairing typefaces, scale, measure and leading, web fonts, and the foundries worth watching. Read Butterick first (fast rules), then Rutter (the same craft applied to responsive web), then keep Bringhurst as the reference.

## Start here
- **Butterick's Practical Typography, "Typography in ten minutes":** five rules that fix most bad body text, free online (opinion).
- **Richard Rutter, Web Typography (and webtypography.net):** the classical rules translated into CSS and responsive layout (opinion).
- **Google Fonts Knowledge, "Choosing type" and "Using type":** free, expert-written lessons on selection, pairing, line length, line height and web font loading (opinion).

## Entries

### The Elements of Typographic Style, Robert Bringhurst (book)
- **Who / what:** the standard reference on typographic style from poet and typographer Robert Bringhurst; Hartley & Marks, fourth edition (version 4.2, 2016, twentieth anniversary edition).
- **Best for:** settling detail questions with authority: measure, word space, hyphenation, figures, small caps, scale, page proportion.
- **Why it meets the bar:** Butterick's bibliography calls it a standard reference among professional typographers, and Rutter built a whole web guide on it. Opinion: a reference to consult, not a first read for beginners.
- **Link:** https://search.worldcat.org/title/The-elements-of-typographic-style/oclc/967670384
- **Tier:** 3
- **Checked:** WorldCat record (fourth edition, version 4.2, Hartley & Marks, 2016), Open Library edition list (versions 2.4 to 4.1); accessed 2026-10-10. Already used in kb/research/typography/01 and 06.

### Butterick's Practical Typography, Matthew Butterick (book, free online)
- **Who / what:** a web book by a writer, lawyer and type designer (Harvard visual-studies degree) who drew the fonts the book is set in.
- **Best for:** fast, concrete rules on point size, line spacing, line length, font choice, headings, and when to use bold, caps or underline; "Typography in ten minutes" is the entry point.
- **Why it meets the bar:** the author designs and sells his own typefaces (MB Type), so the advice comes from shipped type work; the book is maintained online. Opinion: the best ratio of rules to reading time in the field.
- **Link:** https://practicaltypography.com/
- **Tier:** 3
- **Checked:** home, "Typography in ten minutes", introduction, About Matthew Butterick and bibliography pages; accessed 2026-10-10. Already used in kb/research/typography/01, 02, 04, 05.

### Thinking with Type, Ellen Lupton (book)
- **Who / what:** design educator Ellen Lupton's guide to type at the scale of letter, text and grid; Princeton Architectural Press, third edition, revised and expanded (March 2024, 256 pages).
- **Best for:** visual learners: type families, kerning and tracking, alignment, hierarchy, grids, with annotated examples and exercises. The third edition adds 32 pages and more libre and Google fonts.
- **Why it meets the bar:** in print since 2004, now in a third edition by a designer and curator who teaches the subject (the publisher's "definitive guide" line is marketing). Opinion: the best first book for a designer, where Bringhurst is the best reference.
- **Link:** https://papress.com/products/thinking-with-type-3-edition
- **Tier:** 3
- **Checked:** PA Press product page, Google Books and library catalog records (third edition, 2024, ISBN 9781797226828, 255 to 256 pages); accessed 2026-10-10. Not yet used in kb/research.

### Web Typography, Richard Rutter, and webtypography.net (book + free site)
- **Who / what:** cofounder of the design consultancy Clearleft; his 2017 book is a handbook for responsive web typography, and his free site "The Elements of Typographic Style Applied to the Web" maps Bringhurst's rules to CSS.
- **Best for:** turning print rules into CSS: measure in ems, word and letter spacing, numerals, tables built to be read.
- **Why it meets the bar:** A List Apart published excerpts (numerals, tables, 2017); the site is open under Creative Commons and maintained on GitHub. Opinion: the bridge between classic typography and modern CSS.
- **Link:** https://book.webtypography.net/ and https://webtypography.net/
- **Tier:** 3 (book); the free site, tier 4
- **Checked:** webtypography.net introduction (2005 site, 2017 book), sample rule 2.1.2 on measure, A List Apart author page and excerpts, LinkedIn headline confirming Clearleft cofounder; accessed 2026-10-10. The book site sat behind a bot check, so its contents were confirmed through A List Apart. Already used in kb/research/typography/01.

### Google Fonts Knowledge (docs)
- **Who / what:** Google Fonts' library of guides written with outside type experts, in modules: Introducing type, Choosing type, Using type.
- **Best for:** pairing (including the font matrix), x-height, optical sizes, line height, measure, variable fonts, self-hosting and loading web fonts, OpenType features on the web.
- **Why it meets the bar:** official documentation from the largest free font library; lessons are by working type designers and typographers. Opinion: the most useful free curriculum for UI and web type.
- **Link:** https://fonts.google.com/knowledge
- **Tier:** 1 for font technology; tier 4 for style advice
- **Checked:** Knowledge index and the three module pages with their lesson lists; accessed 2026-10-10. Already used in kb/research/typography/02 and 03.

### Pimp my Type, Oliver Schöndorfer (practitioner + newsletter)
- **Who / what:** Austrian typography consultant and UI designer (15+ years) focused on type in digital products; Font Friday newsletter, YouTube channel, type audits.
- **Best for:** UI typography: picking fonts for interfaces, pairing pitfalls, font recommendations (free and paid) in a weekly email.
- **Why it meets the bar:** his font-matrix pairing method is cited in Google Fonts Knowledge; he has spoken at SmashingConf; client work includes the City of Vienna site (per a recommendation on his LinkedIn). Opinion: the best working voice on UI type specifically.
- **Link:** https://pimpmytype.com/ (newsletter: https://pimpmytype.com/newsletter). His bio says he left X, so there is no X account to list.
- **Tier:** 4
- **Checked:** home page (featured-on list linking Google Fonts Knowledge and SmashingConf), bio page, newsletter page (3,200+ subscribers); accessed 2026-10-10. Already used in kb/research/typography/01, 02, 04.

### Typewolf, Jeremiah Shoaf (gallery)
- **Who / what:** independent typography site, updated daily since June 2013: a type-focused "Site of the Day", 1,000+ font pages with alternatives and pairings, and curated lists.
- **Best for:** seeing a typeface in real shipped sites before choosing it; finding free alternatives to commercial fonts; a weekly Tuesday email.
- **Why it meets the bar:** a long-running, single-curator archive of live examples (350,000+ monthly visitors, per the site). Note: lists carry affiliate links, so treat "best of" lists as opinion.
- **Link:** https://www.typewolf.com/ (X: https://x.com/typewolf, lead only, tier 5)
- **Tier:** 4
- **Checked:** About page (author, launch date, @typewolf handle, affiliate disclosure), Site of the Day archive; accessed 2026-10-10. Already used in kb/research/typography/02.

### Fonts In Use (gallery)
- **Who / what:** a public, independent archive of typographic design indexed by typeface, format, industry and period; blog from 2010, full collection since 2012.
- **Best for:** research by typeface ("where has this been used, and how?"), by industry, or by era; pairing evidence from real work.
- **Why it meets the bar:** the largest searchable index of real typographic work. Opinion: better than any pairing generator because every entry is shipped work.
- **Link:** https://fontsinuse.com/
- **Tier:** 4 (shipped examples, curated by editors from public submissions)
- **Checked:** home and About pages; accessed 2026-10-10. Already used in kb/research/typography/02.

### Utopia fluid type and space calculators (tool)
- **Who / what:** free calculators by James Gilyead and Trys Mudford (Clearleft) that turn two modular scales (one at a small viewport, one at a large one) into CSS `clamp()` steps for type and spacing.
- **Best for:** a fluid type scale (for example 1.2 on mobile to 1.25 on desktop) and a matching space scale, with ready-to-paste CSS custom properties.
- **Why it meets the bar:** the method is documented in their blog and showcase, and the output is inspectable CSS. Opinion: the cleanest way to stop hand-tuning breakpoints for font sizes.
- **Link:** https://utopia.fyi/type/calculator
- **Tier:** 4
- **Checked:** type, space and clamp calculators and their credits, blog post "Designing with fluid type scales"; accessed 2026-10-10. Already used in kb/research/typography/01 and layout/02.

### Klim Type Foundry, Kris Sowersby (foundry to follow)
- **Who / what:** independent foundry in Wellington, New Zealand, led by Kris Sowersby; retail fonts include Söhne, Domaine, National 2 and Financier.
- **Best for:** learning how great typefaces are made and why: long "design information" essays per family (Financier was drawn for the Financial Times redesign; Söhne reworks Akzidenz-Grotesk as used in the NYC Subway signage); judging quality by example.
- **Why it meets the bar:** inspectable shipped work at the top of the field (Financial Times commission, Red Dot recognition). Opinion: reading Klim's essays teaches more about type quality than most books. It also stands in for "foundries worth following"; see dropped list for others.
- **Link:** https://klim.co.nz/blog/ (active on Mastodon at https://typo.social/@klim and Instagram, not X)
- **Tier:** 4
- **Checked:** Klim home and blog index (about 115,000 words of essays, Söhne, Financier and National 2 design-information posts), Red Dot project page; accessed 2026-10-10. Not yet used in kb/research.

### Font In Logo (identification tool)
- **Who / what:** A searchable index of the fonts behind famous brand logos and film, TV and game titles, with "verified" matches and free alternatives.
- **Best for:** answering "what face is that logo set in" fast during brand research or a competitor wall.
- **Why it meets the bar:** has a verified, source-checked tier. Opinion: many entries name a free look-alike (for example Orbitron for Star Wars) rather than the real, often custom, face; trust only the verified tier and confirm on the foundry's or the brand's own page. Fonts In Use stays the better source for type in context.
- **Link:** https://www.fontinlogo.com/
- **Tier:** 5 (verified entries 4)
- **Checked:** home page, the verified filter and sample entries. Lead from the Bardhyl Bytyqi X post. Accessed 2026-10-23.

## Considered and dropped
- **Tim Brown, Flexible Typesetting, and other A Book Apart titles:** well regarded, but not verified this session, so left out.
- **Other foundries (Commercial Type, Grilli Type, Dinamo, Pangram Pangram and others):** worth browsing, but Klim is the one with a deep, public essay archive. Typewolf's "24 independent type foundries" post is a better list than this file could be.
- **Font pairing generators and "top 50 fonts" listicles:** no evidence, no context. Use Fonts In Use or Typewolf instead.
- **Apple HIG and Material type guidance:** tier 1 and already used in kb/research/typography/01 and 04, but they are platform specs. They belong in ux.md and frontend-a11y.md, not in a craft reading list.
- **MDN font docs:** the right source for CSS property behavior (already used heavily in the research notes), but they are reference pages, not a guide to type decisions.
