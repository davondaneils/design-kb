# Strategy and brand

Where to go deeper on brand strategy, positioning and identity systems. Read Wheeler for the process, Sharp and Romaniuk for the evidence, then study shipped identity work at Pentagram and COLLINS. Positioning and offer strategy are already covered by Davon's website-strategy skill (Ries, Dunford, Enns); this list fills what that skill lacks: the design side of strategy and the research on how brands actually grow.

## Start here
- **Designing Brand Identity (Wheeler and Meyerson):** the most complete map of the branding process, phase by phase, with 50+ real case studies. Opinion: read first.
- **How Brands Grow and Building Distinctive Brand Assets (Ehrenberg-Bass):** the evidence for why consistency and distinctive assets matter, which is the business case for every identity rule in this KB. Opinion.
- **Pentagram work archive:** the strongest single place to see strategy turned into shipped identity systems. Opinion.

## Entries

### Designing Brand Identity, 6th edition (book)
- **Who / what:** Alina Wheeler and Rob Meyerson, Wiley, 2024, 352 pages. A spread-per-topic guide to brand fundamentals, strategy and process.
- **Best for:** structuring a brand project end to end (research, strategy, identity design, touchpoints, managing assets) and finding a real case study for almost any identity problem.
- **Why it meets the bar:** in its sixth edition; Wiley-VCH notes it was the first book to break branding into a universal five-phase method and is published in 11 languages. The 6th edition adds 50+ case studies from leading studios. Opinion: the standard textbook for brand teams.
- **Link:** https://www.wiley.com/en-us/designing-brand-identity-a-comprehensive-guide-to-the-world-of-brands-and-branding-6th-edition-p-9781119984818
- **Tier:** 3
- **Checked:** Wiley product page (authors, ISBN 978-1-119-98481-8, March 2024, 352 pages) and Wiley-VCH page (five-phase method, 11 languages), accessed 2026-10-10.

### How Brands Grow, and Building Distinctive Brand Assets (book / research)
- **Who / what:** Byron Sharp (How Brands Grow, Oxford University Press, 2010) and Jenni Romaniuk (Building Distinctive Brand Assets; co-author of How Brands Grow Part 2), both of the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, University of South Australia.
- **Best for:** defending consistency in an identity system with evidence: mental availability, physical availability and how to pick and measure distinctive assets (colors, shapes, characters, logos).
- **Why it meets the bar:** OUP describes the book as built on replicated, generalized findings ("laws") rather than opinion. Romaniuk developed the Distinctive Asset Grid used by companies to assess asset strength, and has published in journals such as the Journal of Business Research. Opinion: the best counterweight to taste-only brand advice.
- **Link:** https://global.oup.com/academic/product/how-brands-grow-9780195573565 ; http://www.jenniromaniuk.com/ ; institute: https://marketingscience.info/
- **Tier:** 3
- **Checked:** OUP product page (author, 2010, ISBN 9780195573565), Romaniuk biography page (Distinctive Asset Grid, journal record), Ehrenberg-Bass home page, accessed 2026-10-10.

### The Long and the Short of It (research report)
- **Who / what:** Les Binet and Peter Field for the IPA, 2013. Meta-analysis of the IPA Effectiveness Databank: 996 campaigns, 700 brands, 83 categories.
- **Best for:** the balance between brand building and short-term activation (the "60:40" finding), and why emotional, fame-seeking brand work pays back over time.
- **Why it meets the bar:** large dataset, published by the industry body, followed by later IPA reports (Media in Focus 2017, Effectiveness in Context 2018). The 60:40 ratio is a databank average, not a law; context changes it. Opinion: essential context when a client asks "why spend on brand at all".
- **Link:** https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/effectiveness-research-analysis/les-binet-peter-field (summary deck: https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/documents/the-long-and-the-short-of-it-presentation)
- **Tier:** 3 (industry databank research, not peer reviewed)
- **Checked:** IPA key-works page and presentation page (10 principles), IPA presentation PDF (996 campaigns, 700 brands, 83 categories), accessed 2026-10-10.

### The Brand Gap and Zag (book)
- **Who / what:** Marty Neumeier, former Neumeier Design Team (Palo Alto; Adobe, Apple, HP clients) and founder of Critique magazine. The Brand Gap (2003) bridges business strategy and design; Zag covers radical differentiation.
- **Best for:** a fast shared vocabulary with clients: "a brand is what they say it is", the five disciplines, and the "onlyness" test for positioning.
- **Why it meets the bar:** short, widely used books. His site states The Brand Gap slide deck has been viewed over 20 million times and Zag was named one of The 100 Best Business Books of All Time. Opinion: a primer, lighter on evidence than Sharp; use it for framing, not proof.
- **Link:** https://www.martyneumeier.com/the-brand-gap ; https://www.martyneumeier.com/zag ; onlyness test: https://www.martyneumeier.com/the-onlyness-test
- **Tier:** 3 (book), claims are practitioner opinion
- **Checked:** martyneumeier.com book pages and onlyness page; Wikipedia and LinkedIn for career history, accessed 2026-10-10.

### Pentagram (practitioner, studio)
- **Who / what:** partner-owned design consultancy (London, New York and more). Its site describes 23 partners working independently and together; disciplines include brand strategy, brand identity, naming and verbal identity.
- **Best for:** seeing how a strategy becomes a full system: the MIT Media Lab grid-based identity, Paula Scher's 30 summers of Shakespeare in the Park, Emily Oberman's SNL stewardship.
- **Why it meets the bar:** every project page is inspectable shipped work, with a short story of the brief and the system. The KB already relies on its MIT Media Lab case (kb/research/graphic/01-logo-identity.md). Opinion: the deepest public archive of identity work by one firm.
- **Link:** https://www.pentagram.com/work ; brand identity index: https://www.pentagram.com/brand-identity
- **Tier:** 4
- **Checked:** pentagram.com home and About text (23 partners, discipline list), MIT Media Lab story page, accessed 2026-10-10.

### How to... (book) by Michael Bierut
- **Who / what:** Pentagram partner since 1990, AIGA Medal 2006, co-founder of Design Observer. How to use graphic design to sell things, explain things... (Harper Design, 2015; revised second edition 2021).
- **Best for:** project-by-project reasoning from brief to system: 40+ case studies framed as challenges ("How to design two dozen logos at once" for MIT, "How to keep it simple" for Mastercard).
- **Why it meets the bar:** written by the designer of the work, showing process and client relationships, not just results. Opinion: the best book for learning how identity decisions get argued and sold.
- **Link:** https://www.pentagram.com/news/michael-bierut-s-how-to-updated-with-second-edition ; bio: https://www.pentagram.com/about/michael-bierut
- **Tier:** 3 (book) and 4 (the work)
- **Checked:** Pentagram news page on the second edition, Pentagram bio (AIGA Medal, books), Wikipedia (partner since 1990), accessed 2026-10-10.

### COLLINS (practitioner, studio)
- **Who / what:** independent brand and experience design company in New York and San Francisco, founded by Brian Collins (previously ran Ogilvy & Mather's Brand Integration Group for ten years).
- **Best for:** design-led strategy: the studio lists brand strategy, naming, verbal branding and messaging architecture next to identity and product design. Case studies include Dropbox, Figma, Robinhood, Bose and Target up&up.
- **Why it meets the bar:** inspectable case studies on its site; Fast Company named Brian Collins an American Master of Design (2005); SVA faculty since 2001. Opinion: the clearest current example of strategy and craft from one team. Brand New's 2017 review of the Dropbox identity (headline "Thinking Too Far Outside of the Box") offers a skeptical second view worth reading alongside the case study.
- **Link:** https://wearecollins.com/ ; X: https://x.com/briancollins1 (lead only, tier 5)
- **Tier:** 4
- **Checked:** wearecollins.com home and About (capability list), briancollins.design About, Wikipedia, X profile excerpt (bio links wearecollins.com), Brand New Dropbox review title, accessed 2026-10-10.

### Brand New by UnderConsideration (gallery/teardown)
- **Who / what:** daily reviews of new logo and identity launches by Armin Vit (formerly of Pentagram New York) and Bryony Gomez-Palacio, Bloomington, IN. Also runs the Brand New Conference.
- **Best for:** before/after comparisons of real rebrands and a running record of how identity systems are received. A subscriber collection gathers published brand guidelines.
- **Why it meets the bar:** shows shipped work from many studios side by side. The reviews are opinion; the work shown is the evidence. Already used in kb/research/graphic/01-logo-identity.md. Some full content is subscriber-only.
- **Link:** https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/
- **Tier:** 4 for the work shown, 5 for the critique
- **Checked:** Brand New about text and Guidelines collection page, UnderConsideration About (Armin Vit at Pentagram), accessed 2026-10-10.

### Standards case studies (gallery)
- **Who / what:** Standards, a tool for publishing brand guidelines online, with a public set of case studies of real guidelines (Mailchimp, Cash App, Headspace, RISD).
- **Best for:** seeing how mature brands document a system: tone of voice, illustration, motion and layout rules in one place. Model for how a brand chapter in this KB should be structured.
- **Why it meets the bar:** shows real guideline structure from shipped brands. It is also a vendor showcase, so treat the commentary as marketing. Already used in kb/research/graphic/01-logo-identity.md.
- **Link:** https://standards.site/examples/
- **Tier:** 4
- **Checked:** Mailchimp case study page (contents, links to Cash App, Headspace, RISD), accessed 2026-10-10.
