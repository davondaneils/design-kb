# Halden Hearing: discovery inputs (website-strategy stages 2 to 5, compressed)

Inputs to `strategy.md` at full depth (design-strategy section 1: new identity direction, positioning missing). This is the working file; `strategy.md` is the one-page summary every playbook reads. ## Stage 2: fact sheet
Confirmed (source: brief):
- Identity: Halden Hearing, Clifton, Bristol. Audiologists Priya Nair and Tom Ashby. Opened after both left a national high-street chain in 2024.
- Premises: one clinic room, one soundproof booth, a small shop front.
- Offer: hearing test, free, no obligation; earwax removal £65; hearing aids £1,495 to £3,995 a pair including four years of aftercare; hearing aids from four manufacturers; fittings, adjustments and earwax removal for people who bought aids elsewhere.
- Method: appointments are 90 minutes; no commission.
- Demand: 40 percent of new patients from GP or friend referrals; most of the rest search "hearing test Bristol".
- Current assets: template site, stock photo of an older couple laughing on a beach. No reviews, no photos of the people or the room, no logo worth keeping.
- Competitors named: Specsavers, Boots Hearingcare, Amplifon, two unnamed independents.

Outside facts used (sourced):
- "People wait, on average, 10 years to seek help for their hearing loss" (RNID, Hearing Matters, 2020, citing Davis et al. 2007), https://rnid.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Hearing-Matters-Report.pdf, accessed 2026-10-13. The founders' "about ten years" matches it.
- Age-related hearing loss symptoms include "Problems telling apart certain sounds, such as 's' or 'th'", "Problems hearing in noisy areas", "Having to raise the volume on the TV" (MedlinePlus, Age-related hearing loss, https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001045.htm, accessed 2026-10-13). Consonants "s, t, k, p and f ... as is the th sound" are softer and higher pitched (Todd Ricketts, Vanderbilt, in AARP, https://www.aarp.org/health/conditions-treatments/hearing-decline-age, accessed 2026-10-13). People "think that others seem to mumble" (National Institute on Aging, https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/hearing-and-hearing-loss/hearing-loss-common-problem-older-adults, accessed 2026-10-13).

Gaps, as client questions:
- Critical: none blocks positioning.
- Important: Can we name the four manufacturers? What do fittings and adjustments for aids bought elsewhere cost? Phone number, street address, opening hours (the page uses an Ofcom drama-range number and "Clifton Village, BS8" as stand-ins). Are both of you HCPC registered, and may we print the numbers? Is the 90 minutes the first appointment only, or every appointment? Will you let us photograph you, the booth and the shop front?
- Nice to have: patient words (5 to 10 short interviews), a GP who refers to you and would be quoted.

Schwartz check: (1) a hearing test that takes its time; (2) mechanism: 90 minutes, no commission, any of four manufacturers; (3) proof: published prices, named people, the aftercare term; no reviews yet; (4) people who have put it off for years; (5) hidden benefit: not being made to feel old or sold to.

## Stage 3: ICP
Core complex: fear of losing their place in conversation, and of being sold something expensive the moment they admit it.

Primary avatar (stand-in, built from the brief and the sources above, not from interviews): Ann, 68, retired, lives in Redland. Restaurants and family lunches have become tiring; she nods along. Her daughter keeps saying "you need your ears tested".
2am thought: "I'm not deaf. Everyone mumbles. And if I go to one of those places they'll put a three-thousand-pound thing in my ear before I've had my coat off."
Fears: looking old; being pressured; paying for the wrong device; finding out it is worse than she thought.
Frustrations: chains feel like shops; appointments feel short (brief, client view, unverified).
Awareness: problem aware, partly solution aware (knows free tests exist everywhere). Lead with her situation, then the different kind of appointment.
Benefit ladder: 90-minute test (feature); a clear answer about what she is missing and what it costs (functional); relief, no pressure (emotional); still the one who follows the conversation at Sunday lunch (identity).
Secondary avatars: the adult child booking for a parent (needs prices and the phone number to pass on); the owner of hearing aids bought elsewhere that sit in a drawer (needs "we see you too").
Language mirror (stand-in, from the sources and search): "hearing test Bristol", "everyone mumbles", "I can hear, I just can't make out the words", "the TV's too loud". Avoid: "solutions", "hearing journey", "FREE", "better hearing in a week".

## Stage 4: positioning
Competitive frame tried: "hearing aids" (imports a price and device comparison the chains win on range), "hearing care" (the chains' own word; Boots Hearingcare is a brand name), "independent audiologists for a hearing test in Clifton" (chosen: it is what people search, and its default assumptions are time, impartiality and the same person).
One concept to own: time.
Angles: A, time (90 minutes, no commission): true, exclusive against the chains on our wall, relevant to the core complex, durable while there are two of them, one sentence. B, impartial (four manufacturers): true but the independents can say it. C, second opinion for aids bought elsewhere: true and useful, too narrow for the hero; it becomes a section CEP. Chosen: A.
Internal positioning statement: For people who have put off a hearing test for years, Halden is the independent audiologist in Clifton that gives every appointment 90 minutes and earns no commission, because Priya and Tom left a chain to work that way.
Tagline territory: "Ninety minutes to find the words you've been missing." / "Take 90 minutes." / "A hearing test, not a sales pitch." / "Two audiologists. No commission. No hurry." / "Hear the whole sentence."
Rules out: stock smiling couples, glamour shots of devices, speed promises, "FREE" in capitals, discount banners, any claim about what the chains do.

## Stage 5: brand strategy (what the two built sections must do)
Hero: before "is this another chain?"; after "this is about the words I'm missing, and nobody will rush me". One sentence, the heard and said line, one action, the phone number.
Section 2: before "what will it cost me and who are these people?"; after "I know the prices and the names; I can book". Alternatives and the perfect world (time, no commission, any manufacturer, aftercare included), then the ledger of prices.
Single feeling: being listened to before anyone talks about a device.
