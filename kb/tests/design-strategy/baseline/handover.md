# Halden Hearing: hand-over

## What we decided
- **Positioning:** an independent audiology clinic where the first appointment is 90 minutes, free, with no obligation, and nobody earns commission. Brand idea: "Time, not a sale." It fails the swap test for Specsavers, Boots Hearingcare and Amplifon on three facts (90 minutes, no commission, founders who left a chain).
- **Audience:** the person who has put it off for years and has just been nudged (by a GP, a friend or family) to search "hearing test Bristol", often with an adult child beside them. Secondary: people unhappy with aids bought elsewhere.
- **Voice:** Priya and Tom across the desk. Plain, short sentences, real numbers, no adjectives doing the reassuring.
- **Identity:** book serif headlines (system Charter/Georgia), system sans body at 19 to 20px; warm paper, warm ink and one Bristol-brick accent, all text pairs at WCAG AAA in light and dark; real photography of the founders and room only, never stock; a wordmark plus an open-quarter circle mark direction; a 90-minute line as the graphic device.
- **Home page:** first screen states the feeling, then the facts, then two equal ways in (book or call). The next section shows what the 90 minutes are for, publishes all three prices, and invites people who bought elsewhere.

## What we did not build
- The photo shoot. The hero has a labelled placeholder; launching with stock would undo the positioning.
- A finished logo. The mark is a direction, drawn once in the header.
- Booking flow, About, Find us, earwax removal and adjustment pages. Links are stubs.
- Reviews. We had no real reviews in the brief and did not invent any. Add Google reviews once collected, with names and dates.
- Local SEO work (Google Business Profile, LocalBusiness schema, a dedicated "hearing test Bristol" page). This matters: most non-referral patients arrive from that search.
- Any comparison with named competitors.

## Must verify before launch
- Do the two other Bristol independents also offer long appointments or no commission? If yes, lean harder on the founders' story and on "we look after aids you bought elsewhere".
- The four steps in the 90 minutes are a plain description of a typical full assessment. Priya and Tom must confirm the order and wording, and that "nothing hurts" is how they want to say it.
- The phone number 0117 496 0123 is a fictional placeholder from Ofcom's drama range.
- Confirm whether earwax removal and adjustments are open to non-patients, as the page implies the brief supports.

## What we would test
1. **Headline:** "You've waited long enough. We won't rush you." against a fact-led line, "A 90-minute hearing test. Free. No commission." Watch for anyone reading the first as a scold. Five-second tests with 8 to 10 people over 60, then booking rate.
2. **Book vs call:** share of contacts by phone versus online form. If phone dominates, make the number the primary button on mobile.
3. **Prices on the home page:** does showing £1,495 to £3,995 raise or lower test bookings? We expect it raises trust; measure it.
4. **Referral landing:** a short URL for GPs and a "Sent by a friend?" line, to see whether referred visitors convert faster than search visitors.
5. **Mark direction:** the open-quarter circle against two alternatives (pure wordmark; a monogram H) with patients and referring GPs. Pick on recognition and on "does this look like a chain?"
6. **Readability:** check every key line against a reading age of 9 to 11, and test the page at 200 percent zoom on a phone with older users.
