# Moment study: nine exemplar moments, timed to the frame (2026-10-15)

Why this file exists: the fresh brief (`kb/tests/fresh/RESULTS.md`) reached "close" on motion, and Davon's verdict on the recordings was that the motion was "not anything special". Our checks measure whether motion is present (bands, a scene, holds), not whether a moment is memorable. This file studies the moments people remember on the best pages, at frame level, so the kit (`skills/design-motion/assets/moments.js`) can rebuild them and the blind gate (`skills/design-motion/assets/blind-gate.mjs`) can judge against them.

**Method.** `kb/moments/film.mjs` films a page with a scripted camera (Playwright `recordVideo`, 1440x900, 25 fps H.264); `kb/moments/sheet.sh` turns a clip into a stamped contact sheet (time and frame on every tile); `kb/moments/trace.mjs` samples the computed transform, opacity, clip-path, filter and box of named elements on every animation frame (52 to 56 fps measured) and reports each movement's start, end and length and the holds between them. The library of camera scripts is `kb/moments/library.json` (re-film with `node kb/moments/film-library.mjs`). Earlier whole-page timing comes from `kb/teardowns/data/choreo/*.json` (`kb/teardowns/motion-imagery.md`). Everything below is measured unless marked **opinion**. Sheets are in `kb/moments/sheets/`, traces in `kb/moments/data/`.

The directing vocabulary used below (event shot, what owns the screen, reading hold counted from legibility, 0.1s offsets, a 1.00 to 1.03 push, three stills, four-field corrections) comes from the article linked in Davon's tweet (x.com/skunzu/status/2110097012339200134, article x.com/i/article/2108585693232455681), which wrote it for Remotion launch films. Every one of those ideas shows up in the exemplars below, which is why the kit adopts them.

## What the nine share (measured, then the rule the kit enforces)

1. **One owner at a time.** In every moment exactly one thing moves while the eye is meant to be on it; the next thing waits until the first is legible (Linear 1.1 s, Stripe's browser 150 to 250 ms after the phone, Stripe's hero type while the gradient only drifts). Kit rule: each beat of a shot plan names one `owner`; the check fails a beat with two.
2. **Holds are long, changes are short.** Stripe holds a state 7.5 s and changes it in under 1.1 s; Sonner holds 4 s. Changes are under a second; reading time is the majority of the loop. Kit rule: the hold after a change is counted from the moment the text is legible (the change has finished), at reading-aloud speed (below).
3. **Change what is there, do not cut to something new.** Stripe rolls values in masks; Sonner restacks; Linear sharpens. Nothing cuts. Kit moments `swap` and `stack` do exactly this.
4. **The curve matches the job.** In-place swap: hard ease-in-out `(.78,0,.22,1)`. Enter: strong ease-out (`(.33,1,.68,1)`, `(.215,.61,.355,1)`). Large surface: `(.32,.72,0,1)`. Kit tokens `--ease-swap` and `--ease-sheet` join the existing `--ease-enter`.
5. **Offsets are about a tenth of a second.** Stripe's second group 150 to 250 ms, Linear's subhead about 300 ms, Sonner's restack simultaneous with the entry. The article's starting value is 0.1 s between a headline and its label. Kit rule: offsets between related elements 80 to 250 ms.
6. **The product's own gesture is the climax.** Raycast presses its hotkey; Stripe's checkout changes country. **Opinion:** our fresh-brief scenes animated a bar filling; nobody remembers a bar filling.
7. **Exits are faster than entrances** (Sonner 200 against 400 ms), as FOUNDATIONS already says for exit easing.

## Reading time (the hold rule)

Adults read silently at about 238 words a minute and aloud at about 183 (Brysbaert 2019, "How many words do we read per minute? A review and meta-analysis of reading rate", *Journal of Memory and Language* 109). The article's test is "play at normal speed and read aloud; extend any hold that ends before you finish", so the kit uses the aloud rate: **hold = max(1000 ms, 300 ms + 330 ms per word)**, counted from when the text is legible, a number or price counting as one word. Stripe's 7.5 s holds clear this for every state (the longest phone state is about 14 words: 4.9 s).

## What we rebuild (the kit)

Three moments are rebuilt closely in `skills/design-motion/assets/moments.js` and shown working in `skills/design-motion/assets/fixtures/moments-demo.html`:
- **swap** (moment 1 and 9): values roll inside masks on `(.78,0,.22,1)`, cascading top to bottom, a following group offset by 150 ms, holds from the plan.
- **stack** (moment 7): items enter from 100% of their height; older items step back scale 0.95 and -14px per level; three visible.
- **event shot** (moments 5 and 3, plus the article's method): a cursor travels on a spring to the control, the press registers (scale 0.97 for one press length), the result appears and owns the screen for its reading hold, with an optional 1.00 to 1.03 push.

The other six stay as references in this file and in design-motion `references/09-moments-and-directing.md`.
