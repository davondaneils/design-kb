# Brief: 02 Proof and trust design

**Question:** Which proof (reviews, ratings, logos, numbers, guarantees, security cues) goes where, and in what form, so it convinces a careful buyer without making a premium brand look cheap?

**Audience:** Claude as art director on a landing, product or pricing page.

**Decisions the reader must be able to make**
- Which proof type answers which doubt (quality, fit, risk, safety of payment).
- Where each proof sits relative to the claim and the CTA.
- How to show a rating: average, count, distribution, and whether to show negative reviews.
- When a trust seal belongs (payment fields) and when it cheapens (hero, nav).
- How to write and set a guarantee as a policy, not a badge.

**Fundamentals (gate)**
- Proof is real and attributable; no invented reviews, insider reviews without disclosure, or suppressed negatives (FTC final rule, Aug 2024).
- A rating always shows its count (Baymard, product ratings perception).
- Proof sits next to the claim it proves (playbook-landing non-negotiable 3).
- Stars and bars never carry meaning by color alone; the number is in text (FOUNDATIONS item 11).

**Expert layer**
- Proof with a denominator: "4.7 from 1,284" beats "4.9"; 4.0 to 4.7 sells better than 5.0 (Spiegel Research Center).
- Show a mixed review and the reply: 53% of test users sought negatives (Baymard).
- First-party verifiable facts (lot number, roast date, price paid) as proof on premium sites; badges only at the card field (Baymard perceived security).
- A guarantee with a mechanism and a time ("tell us within 30 days, the next bag is free"), set in body type beside the CTA.

**Must cover:** proof inventory by doubt, placement map, rating component spec, guarantee spec, what reads as cheap and why.

**Out of scope or reuse:** testimonial copy craft (copywriting for direct response; design-copy for premium), review schema markup (design-search 03).

**Test briefs**
1. Coffee subscription section: add ratings and a guarantee. Playbook landing. Exemplar: apple.com product pages, family.co.
2. B2B SaaS homepage with customer logos and one case number. Playbook landing. Exemplar: stripe.com, ramp.com.

**Rubric:** gate first. Then: every rating shows count and distribution; at least one proof element sits within one screen of the primary CTA; no badge or seal outside payment context; guarantee states mechanism and time.
