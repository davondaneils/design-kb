# Brief: 01 CTA hierarchy and placement

**Question:** How many actions does a view offer, in what order of emphasis, where do they sit, and what do they say?

**Audience:** Claude as art director through design-director and design-playbook-landing (or -dashboard for in-app actions).

**Decisions the reader must be able to make**
- Which single action is primary per view, and which are secondary or tertiary, as tokens (filled, outlined, text link).
- Where the primary repeats down a long page, and where it must not.
- The label: verb plus object, matching what happens next; when to add the price or term to the label.
- Whether a phone page gets a sticky CTA, and when it appears.
- The low-commitment alternative beside a high-commitment primary (subscribe vs buy one).

**Fundamentals (gate)**
- One filled primary per view; same verb everywhere it appears (playbook-landing non-negotiable 1; FOUNDATIONS item 12).
- Label says what happens next; no generic "Get started" when the next step is a quiz, a form or a payment (NN/g, Get Started Stops Users).
- Primary visible in the first screen at 390 and 1440 (NN/g scrolling: 57% of viewing time above the fold).
- Touch target 44px on the primary at 390; focus ring; contrast (foundations).

**Expert layer**
- Repeat the primary only at decision points (after price, after proof), not after every section.
- The commitment ladder: pair a big commitment with a smaller one, set as secondary, so the visitor never leaves to think.
- Price or term inside the button when the button commits money ("Start for $18 a bag").
- Sticky phone CTA only after the inline one scrolls out, and never over form fields or the cookie bar.

**Must cover:** emphasis tokens, placement map for a landing page, labels, sticky rules, dashboard header primary.

**Out of scope or reuse:** button states and craft (design-polish 01), button copy voice (design-copy 02), funnel step order (sales-funnels).

**Test briefs**
1. Coffee roaster landing page (slice brief A): subscription primary, single bag secondary. Playbook landing. Exemplar: apple.com product page buy flow, mercury.com.
2. SaaS pricing page with three plans and a free tier. Playbook landing. Exemplar: linear.app/pricing, vercel.com/pricing.
3. Dashboard header with Share as primary and an upgrade path that must not compete. Playbook dashboard. Exemplar: Vercel Dashboard.

**Rubric:** FOUNDATIONS gate first. Then: exactly one filled primary per viewport screenshot; every CTA label predicts the next screen; primary in first screen at both widths; the secondary is a real lower-commitment path, not a duplicate.
