# Brief: Readability and scannability

**Domain:** copy. **Skill:** design-copy.

## Question
How do you write so people who scan, skim and read on phones still get the message?

## Decisions the reader must be able to make
- Target reading grade for the audience.
- Sentence, paragraph and page length limits.
- Heading and link wording that survives scanning.
- When to use lists, tables, bold and numerals.

## Fundamentals (non-negotiable)
- Front-load: the first two words of a heading, link or list item carry the meaning.
- Plain words; explain specialist terms on first use.
- Sentences over 25 words get split; one idea per paragraph.
- Headings are informative statements or questions, not labels or puns.

## Expert layer
- Numbers: 79% scan (1997), 20 to 28% of words read, 10 to 20 second dwell.
- Grade 5 to 7 copy converts best in Unbounce's dataset (correlation).
- Experts want plain language too; grade 10 to 12 ceiling for experts.
- Requirement verbs (must, need, can).

## Must cover
Inverted pyramid, headings, links, lists, numerals, abbreviations, all caps, italics, measure hand-off to typography.

## Out of scope or reuse
Measure, line height and type size: design-typography. SEO titles and snippets: design-search.

## Test briefs
- "Rewrite this 300 word pricing FAQ so people actually read it."
