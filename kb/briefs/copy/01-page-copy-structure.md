# Brief: Page copy structure (hero, sections, CTAs, copy with layout)

**Domain:** copy. **Skill:** design-copy. **Audience:** a designer writing and laying out websites and landing pages for themselves and clients.

## Question
What does each block of a marketing page have to say, in what order, and how long can each piece of copy be in its layout slot?

## Decisions the reader must be able to make
- What the hero headline, subheadline and primary CTA say, and how to test them before shipping.
- The section order for a homepage, a product page and a single-offer landing page.
- How many words each slot can hold, and what to cut when the layout and the copy fight.
- What every CTA and link label says.

## Fundamentals (non-negotiable)
- The hero says what the thing is and who it is for, in words a visitor understands with no prior context.
- Every claim is specific and checkable; no slogans that a competitor could also sign.
- CTA labels name the outcome; no "Learn more", "Click here", "Submit" as primary actions.
- Sentence case, no orphaned jargon, real copy in the layout (never lorem ipsum at review).

## Expert layer
- The 10 second window and the "only this text" litmus test.
- Message layer order (clarity, relevance, value, differentiation) as a diagnostic: fix the earliest failed layer first.
- Character budgets per slot tied to the shared type scale and measure.
- Objection mapping: one major objection in the hero, others in feature blocks and FAQ.

## Must cover
Hero anatomy, section sequences, feature blocks, social proof placement, CTA ladders, FAQ, footer, link labels, layout fit.

## Out of scope or reuse
Persuasion psychology, awareness levels, long-form sales letters: `copywriting`. Positioning and ICP: `website-strategy`. Funnel steps and offers: `sales-funnels`. Visual hierarchy: design-layout. Type sizes: design-typography.

## Test briefs
- "Write the hero and section outline for a B2B SaaS that automates invoice matching for accountants."
- "Our homepage says 'Empowering teams to do their best work'. Fix it."
