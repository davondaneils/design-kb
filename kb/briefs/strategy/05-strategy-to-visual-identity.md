# Brief 05: Strategy to visual identity

## Question
How does each strategic attribute decide type, color, imagery, mark, layout and motion, with a stated reason and evidence level, and which skill builds each decision?

## Audience
Claude filling the identity decision table of the strategy brief, before design-typography, design-color, design-art-direction and design-graphic start.

## Decisions the reader must be able to make
- Write one row per decision: attribute, choice, reason, evidence level (E evidence, C convention, O opinion), owner skill.
- Choose the primary distinctive asset (usually a shape) and its supporting assets.
- Map the category codes and decide per code: keep or break.
- For a rebrand, audit existing assets on fame and uniqueness before changing anything.
- Hand the concept sentence to design-art-direction.

## Fundamentals
Every decision has a reason and an evidence label; folklore is never evidence; brand never overrides contrast or the accessibility floor; tokens from FOUNDATIONS.

## Expert layer
Shape beats color as an asset; copy outranks shape; keep semantic codes, break one expressive code; Use-or-lose assets survive a rebrand; Binet and Field for why distinctive, emotional brand work matters.

## Must cover
Romaniuk's grid and the 2026 benchmark; Jiang 2016 logo shape; bouba/kiki 2022; Brumberger 2003; Monzo, Mastercard, Tropicana cases.

## Out of scope or reuse
Building the palette, faces, mark and imagery (owner skills). Color evidence (design-color psychology-brand).

## Test briefs
1. New brand for a precise B2B tool. 2. Rebrand of a regional institution with a known symbol.

## Rubric
Every row traceable; at least one non-color distinctive asset; codes table present; rebrand has the asset audit.
