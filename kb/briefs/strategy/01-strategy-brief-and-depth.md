# Brief 01: The strategy brief and how deep to go

## Question
What does the one-page strategy brief contain, and how much strategy does this job need: three lines, one page, or the full process with website-strategy?

## Audience
Claude acting as design director, right after design-foundations loads and before any playbook decision, on any page, screen or brand job.

## Decisions the reader must be able to make
- Pick the depth from the brief in one step: inherit (screen inside an existing product), three lines (campaign, event, one-off artifact), one page (new page or site for a brand with no strategy on file), full (new brand, rebrand, brand system, or positioning missing).
- Know when to hand off to website-strategy's six stages (a client proposal, a freelance job post, a new brand with no facts) and what to take back from it.
- Fill the template fields in order and know which skill reads each field.
- State what was not done and why ("Not built: brand strategy document; the brief did not ask").

## Fundamentals
No visual decision before the brief at the right depth exists. Depth follows the brief, never the skill (design-playbook-brand section 0). The brief fits one page. Opinion labelled.

## Expert layer
Never rebrand by accident inside a screen job; the brief's fields are the contract the playbook and references consume; the three-line brief as the default for most work.

## Must cover
Wheeler's five phases as the full process map; the depth table; the template; hand-off map to website-strategy stages 2 to 6.

## Out of scope or reuse
The six stages themselves (website-strategy). Proposal writing (website-strategy stage 6).

## Test briefs
1. "Poster and one-page site for a two-day print festival" (three-line depth). 2. "Rebrand our 40-year-old credit union" (full depth with asset audit). 3. "Add a settings screen to our app" (inherit).

## Rubric
Depth chosen matches the table; nothing built beyond the brief's deliverables; the brief fits one page; every field a playbook reads is filled.
