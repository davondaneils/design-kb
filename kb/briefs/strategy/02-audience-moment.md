# Brief 02: Audience as a person in a moment

## Question
Who is the page for, described as a situation they are in (trigger, push, anxiety, words), so that design decisions can be traced to it?

## Audience
Claude writing the audience line of the strategy brief before layout and copy.

## Decisions the reader must be able to make
- Write the audience as a moment: who, in what situation, after what trigger, wanting what progress, held back by what.
- Name the category entry points (CEPs) the brand should own, and pick the primary one for the hero.
- List anxieties and habits with the design answer for each (proof placement, guarantee, price shown, sample data).
- Collect the buyer's own words for the hero and the category word.
- Set the awareness level (via website-strategy stage 3) and note market sophistication (via copywriting) where it changes the hero.

## Fundamentals
Never demographics alone. Status quo and anxieties named. Words come from customers, not the brand.

## Expert layer
CEPs (Romaniuk) as the bridge from audience to message; the four forces (Moesta), anxiety reduction beating added pull; the 95:5 rule (Dawes) for who sees most brand work.

## Must cover
CEP definition and how to list them; four forces; how each maps to a section or slot.

## Out of scope or reuse
Core complex, avatar, benefit ladder, objection map: website-strategy stage 3. Awareness levels: website-strategy stage 3. Sophistication: copywriting.

## Test briefs
1. Bookkeeping app for UK freelancers. 2. Dental clinic booking site.

## Rubric
The moment is a situation; at least one anxiety has a named design answer; the hero uses the audience's words.
