# Brief 01: Concept and consistency

## Question
What is this site's one visual idea, and how do I make every image, texture and graphic on every page obey it?

## Audience
Claude acting as art director on a landing page, product site or app, after positioning is known (website-strategy) and before layout.

## Decisions the reader must be able to make
- Name the concept in one sentence that a stranger could use to reject an image.
- Derive the concept from the brand's own facts (product, process, place, data), not from a mood word.
- Turn the concept into rules: a visual vocabulary (3 to 5 recurring motifs), one treatment, a palette subset, a crop family, and where imagery is banned.
- Decide which sections get imagery and which get none.
- Audit a set for consistency and fix outliers.

## Fundamentals
Every image has a job; the concept never overrides contrast, alt text, color-alone or reduced motion; one treatment per set.

## Expert layer
Concept from a true fact, the reject test, recurring motif instead of recurring picture, designing the set rather than the image, the concept reaching dark theme and small states (empty, 404, OG image).

## Must cover
What art direction is (Clarke; Santa Maria via Zeldman), a concept template, a consistency audit, worked examples for a physical-product brand and for a SaaS product.

## Out of scope or reuse
Brand positioning (website-strategy); logo (design-graphic 01); illustration style spec fields (design-graphic 04, reuse, do not restate).

## Test briefs
1. Coffee roaster landing page (slice brief A), light and dark. Playbook: design-playbook-landing. Exemplars: apple.com product pages, family.co.
2. Analytics dashboard (slice brief B). Playbook: design-playbook-dashboard. Exemplars: Linear app, Stripe Dashboard.

## Rubric
Gate: FOUNDATIONS checklist 1 to 15. Then: the concept is stateable in one sentence and visible in at least three places; no image fails the concept's reject test; the dark theme carries the concept (not a dimmed copy).
