# Research 01: Logo and identity

Status key: **C** consensus, **X** contested, **O** one expert's or practitioner opinion.

## Key findings

| # | Claim | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "A logo doesn't sell (directly), it identifies." "A logo is rarely a description of a business." | Paul Rand, Logos, Flags, and Escutcheons (1991) [R1] | C among identity designers |
| 2 | "A logo derives its meaning from the quality of the thing it symbolizes, not the other way around." Meaning arrives by association over time. | Rand [R1] | C |
| 3 | Complexity "harbors a self-destruct mechanism"; "Simplicity is difficult to achieve, yet worth the effort." | Rand [R1] | C |
| 4 | A memorable identity is "appropriate, flexible, and distinguished by its originality"; should be "devoid of fashion or trend." | Ivan Chermayeff, Print interview [R2] | O (canonical studio) |
| 5 | A mark "has to be unusual in some way, even awkward sometimes, in order to hook the viewer" while staying "simple and uncomplicated in form." | Sagi Haviv [R2] | O (canonical studio) |
| 6 | Marks relying on "shading and other graphic niceties" struggle across media; simple concept-driven marks "work best in a wide range of media." | Tom Geismar [R2] | C |
| 7 | Simple marks "thrive" at tiny sizes such as favicons and app icons. | Haviv [R2] | C |
| 8 | Idea first, computer second: technology is "a tool but not the generator of ideas." | Haviv [R2] | O |
| 9 | Process used by a leading mark designer: many fast sketches, refine by tracing, then build in vector on a grid with anchor points aligned; grids keep proportions consistent; adjust by eye where geometry looks wrong. | George Bokhua, Principles of Logo Design (Quarto 2022), via review [R3] | O (respected practitioner) |
| 10 | Optical corrections: correct by eye over strict geometry; round forms overshoot flat ones; reversed (white on dark) marks look heavier (irradiation) and need thinning in a separate artwork. | Logo Geek (Ian Paget) [R4]; overshoot in type design [R5] | C |
| 11 | Overshoot amount: type designers commonly overshoot rounds by roughly 1 to 3 percent of the height (varies by design). | Wikipedia, Overshoot (typography) [R5] | C (range is approximate) |
| 12 | Responsive logos: as space shrinks, remove detail until "only the essence remains"; demonstrated by Joe Harrison's Responsive Logos project. | mne.today on Harrison [R6] | C in practice |
| 13 | Logos are exempt from WCAG contrast (1.4.3 and 1.4.11), but if a logo acts as a control, provide a higher contrast variant or added contrasting element; the exemption does not cover low contrast that is an author choice rather than a brand requirement. | W3C Understanding 1.4.11 [R7] | C (standard) |
| 14 | Dynamic identities are a valid choice for some organizations (MIT Media Lab system generated from a grid). | Pentagram MIT Media Lab case study [R8]; Dezeen [R9] | C as a pattern, O on when |
| 15 | Brand guidelines in the wild define clear space as a unit taken from the mark (for example height of a letter) and minimum sizes in px and mm. | Bank of Canada corporate identifier [R10]; NinjaOne brand [R11] | C |

## Numbers
- Favicon floor: 16x16 CSS px rendering, 32x32 ICO file (see research 06). A mark that fails at 16px fails as a favicon.
- Apple app icon master 1024x1024; Android adaptive layer 108dp with a 66dp safe zone (research 02).
- Overshoot of rounds: about 1 to 3 percent of height [R5].

## Conflicting views
- Symbol vs wordmark for new brands. Haviv argues for simple distinctive symbols; many practitioners argue a young brand cannot afford to teach a symbol and should lead with the name. Treat as judgment: the name must be learned first either way. (O)
- Dynamic identities: praised for flexibility, criticized for weak recognition when the variable part is the mark itself. (X)
- Golden ratio: Bokhua uses it selectively and says simpler proportions often work better [R3]; claims that great logos are built on it are mostly post-rationalization. (X)

## Practitioner tier (leads, labeled as opinion)
- Richard Baird, LogoArchive (active on X): archive of modernist trademarks; useful for reduction and figure-ground references [R12].
- Brand New by UnderConsideration: running critique of identity launches [R13].
- Standards.site and Standards Manual: brand guidelines examples and reissued manuals (NASA, MTA) [R14][R15].
- David Airey resource list (Logo Design Love author) [R16]; toools.design list for brand designers [R17].
- Brett Williams (DesignJoy, on X): productized design service; his public posts focus on speed, focus and taste in client work rather than logo theory. Not used as a source for specs. [R18]
- George Bokhua (on X and Dribbble): grid-built marks, heavy sketching.
