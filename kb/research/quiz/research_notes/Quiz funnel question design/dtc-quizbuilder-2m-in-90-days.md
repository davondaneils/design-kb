# Davon's resource: @DTC_Quizbuilder "$2M in 90 days with Quiz Funnels"

Saved 2026-10-29. Pasted by Davon in the funnel thread. Source: The Funnel Professor (@DTC_Quizbuilder) on X. Not verified against the original post; every figure is the author's self-reported claim, ecommerce (DTC) context.

## Claims and tactics, as stated

- One change, a quiz before the product page, lifted cold traffic conversion from 1.2% to 4.7%; $2M in 90 days.
- What a quiz does that a product page can't: micro-commitments, belief seeding before price, objection pre-handling through the questions, data collection for retargeting.
- "Drop-off diagnosis": track completion per question. Their Q4 ("What solutions have you tried before?") had long options and too many choices. Cut to 4 options max plus "None of the above"; drop-off on that question fell from 22% to 9%; overall completion rose from 19% to 25%.
- "Low-friction entry": Bioma, Nooro, Lunavia, SkinnyFit all open on age or gender. Zero cognitive load, starts the yes-chain, implies tailoring. Aspirational "what do you want to achieve" comes at Q3 or Q4.
  - Sequence: 1 age range, 2 gender, 3 goals/desires, 4 pain points/struggles, 5+ deeper diagnosis.
- "Captive attention" loading screen: 10 to 15 seconds showing a big before/after testimonial, a social proof stat, and a product preview so the offer feels familiar.
- Segment and retarget non-buyers by their answers (struggle, past solutions tried, symptoms), same product, angle matched to what they said.
- Starter quiz (note: contradicts the age-first sequence above): Q1 aspirational goal (multi-select), Q2 main struggle, Q3 past solutions tried, Q4 symptoms (multi-select), Q5-6 demographics, results page, offer.
- Drop-off benchmark: Q1 30 to 40% is normal (biggest filter); each later question under 15%; overall completion 25%+. Any single question at 10%+ drop-off is the one to fix.
- Prompt idea: 8 questions, 4 to 5 options each, a social proof "breather" slide after Q4, an urgency slider after Q7.

## Notes for Stand Firm (opinion)

- The two posts by the same author disagree on Q1 (age first vs goal first). Our cover already does the warm-up job, so the open question is whether Q1 is an easy type question or age.
- "Pain points/symptoms" must be adapted to the brand rule: sell the reward, never the pain. Ask what he wants stronger, not what is wrong with him.
- "Urgency slider" is fine as a self-rated commitment question; no fake scarcity.
- Before/after testimonials on the loading screen: no stock photos of men, and only real testimonials.
