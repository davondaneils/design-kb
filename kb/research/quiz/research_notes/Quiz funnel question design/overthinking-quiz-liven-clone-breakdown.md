# Davon's resource: "Overthinking score" quiz breakdown (Liven-style)

Saved 2026-10-29. Pasted by Davon in the funnel thread, author not named in the paste. Clone template mentioned: https://some-moles-like.clarflow.com/self-discovery-assessment (not opened). Claims are the author's; nothing here is verified.

## As stated

- Positioning is the most important part. The quiz needs a promised outcome, like an advertorial's open loop. Example promise: "Find your overthinking score and stop it for all."
- Structure is near identical to Liven's quiz. 30 questions, zero mention of the product.
- 5-stage funnel: Self-label, Pain deepen, Cost expand, Hope reframe, Future cast.
  1. Self-label (Q1 to 7): gender, age, "How often do you overthink?" By Q7 the user has said "always" and "as long as I can remember". Commitment chain started.
  2. Agreement barrage (Q8 to 17): agree-scale statements ("I find myself stuck on the same thoughts", "I imagine every possible scenario that could go wrong"). Ten in a row where the honest answer is "that's me".
  3. Cost expansion (Q18 to 19): how it affects your career. "Pain in your head is ignorable. Pain in your wallet is not."
  4. Hope injection: interstitial right after, "87% of overthinkers are more empathetic and intelligent." The flaw becomes a superpower that needs the right tool.
  5. Pseudo-diagnosis result: "Overthinking score: 32.33, RED", type "Worrier", duration, impact areas, self-doubt level, clinical visuals, alarming colours.
- Pattern: Hook, Label, Deepen, Expand, Reframe, Diagnose, Capture. 3+ minutes of co-creating a "personalized plan" so the email claim feels like picking up something already theirs.
- What to steal, per author: a strong promise immediately; zero-friction demographic openers before emotional content; agree-scale STATEMENTS to confirm, not questions; social proof interstitials between question clusters; expand from feelings to tangible cost before hope; a clinical-looking result page with scores, types and colour-coded severity.

## Notes for Stand Firm (opinion)

- Keep: the promise (our cover already makes one: "Find your strength, and what's holding it back."), statements he confirms on a scale, interstitials between clusters, the hope reframe, a result that feels earned and specific (a type plus a score or profile), the "already yours" email claim.
- Adapt: the pain stages. Davon's brief says sell the reward, never the pain. Run the agreement barrage on strengths ("When things fall apart, I'm the one people call") and ask about the obstacle once, blamelessly. The cost question can be about what's at stake for his family or calling, framed as what grows, not what's ruined.
- Reject: RED alarm colours and a fake clinical diagnosis. That is a deficit brand, and the FTC case against Genesis Tech (MadMuscles) included results that came out the same whatever the user entered. Our result must change with the answers.
- Length: 30 questions works for a high-pain category on warm intent. Our traffic is cold Instagram on phones; the research points to roughly 8 to 12 one-tap screens plus interstitials.
