# Messaging Brief: updated sections

Sections 3, 4, 10 and 11, as changed on 28 Oct 2026 to add the outside enemies. The rest of the brief is unchanged.

## 3. The pain

Use this to understand him, not to sell to him. The rule is **sell the reward, never the pain** \[Strategy, Decisions\], and the web copy must "never shame him or lead with pain or fear" \[Handoff, copy rules\]. Pain can live inside a story. It never sits in the offer.

**The surface problem he'd admit.**

- "I'm not as strong as I should be," in body, faith or discipline. Quiz question 9 lets him say it in his own terms: my body, my faith, leading my home, my discipline, my confidence \[Handoff\].
- "I don't have time." Full-time work, kids' sports, a wife who wants more \[VOC, objections\].
- "I don't know where to begin" with leading his home spiritually \[VOC, Focus on the Family\].

**The deeper problem he wouldn't say out loud.**

- He doesn't think he measures up. "Deep inside, many of us wonder if we actually can measure up to the job" \[VOC\].
- Nobody ever showed him how, and he's ashamed of that \[VOC: "we didn't have a godly father"\].
- He has no one. Nearly 80% have no one they can be truly honest with, and the younger man links that loneliness straight to his private fight \[VOC\].
- He fears that when it counts, he won't be the man who stays. **(My read)** This is why Kolbe (43M views) and Doss (18M) hit so hard: they answer the question he's afraid to ask about himself \[Strategy, outliers\].
- For the younger man, it's lust he can't beat \[VOC\]. **Flag:** you've ruled out any porn or lust content \[Decisions\], so the product will not speak to what VOC calls his biggest fight. That's a fair brand call, but expect some of these men to feel the product skips their real problem.

**The fears from outside** \[VOC, section 2\]:

- That the world is hostile to his faith, and to the people he loves. "It is intimidating to see how the world treats people of faith, and then to almost put a target on my kids' backs."
- "I worry about the world we're raising our kids in." "How do you protect your kids?"
- That Christians are being killed and attacked, at home and abroad, and "that could be us" \[VOC, buying triggers\].
- That one day he looks back and knows he watched his kids, his church and his country get taken, and did nothing. VOC calls this the deepest fear \[VOC, "What we publicly refuse"\].

**What it costs him day to day** \[VOC, sections 1, 4 and 11\]:

- Promises to himself that don't get kept, starting with the morning. Phone before prayer.
- A nagging guilt that he's "simply not doing enough, as a husband, father, friend, neighbour, employee, Christian."
- Sunday's sermon on men stepping up, and Monday the same as last week.
- At home: his wife asks him to lead and he means to. **(My read)** He loses a little of her respect every time he doesn't, and he feels it.
- No brothers to call. Day 1 of Stand Firm asks him to write down how many men would come at 2 a.m., and the program expects that to be "the hardest number you wrote all day" \[Stand Firm, Day 30\].

## 4. The enemy

A whole series exists for them (They Are Coming for Christians, stage 3: "It is still happening, and it could reach the people I love") \[Strategy\], and VOC names them the wolves at the door \[VOC, Part B\]. The inside enemy is the lie he was sold: **"A good Christian is a nice guy, and our history is shameful"** \[Strategy\], answered by "nice is not good" \[VOC\].

## 10. The voice

**How it sounds:** a regular man talking to a friend at a bar. Plain, short, specific, calm. Strength shown as calm, cost and sacrifice, never swagger \[Show Bible, Script Craft, Viewer Filter\]. Premium and serious, like gold leaf on deep blue, not a marketing funnel \[Handoff\].

**Words and lines we always use:**

- "Real Christianity is strong." "What kind of strong are you?" "Comment STRONG." \[Strategy\]
- "Follow Christ. Lead your home." on every ending \[Show Bible\]. "Lead your home" means the home he has or the one he's building.
- "The people you love," "your home," "the men who'd stand with you" so single men and fathers both fit \[Strategy\].
- "Passive" for Christians who don't lead \[Show Bible\].
- "Brother" in DMs, and as the fallback when there's no first name \[Handoff\].
- His own words: lead, protect, provide, stand firm, step up, brothers, men who show up, built, forged, fight, real, strong \[VOC\].
- "Never miss twice." "Stand firm, / Christian Strength" on every email \[Handoff\].
- The four type names exactly as they are: Protector, Builder, Leader, Warrior \[Decisions\].
- Scripture from the ESV \[Decisions\].

**Claims we always make, and how:**

- About him and the reward he earns, never about other men \[Decisions\].
- Every fact checked that day and said no stronger than the source \[Viewer Filter\].
- Real anchors and real scarcity only: $37 regular, $27 for the first 50 men, with a live count \[Handoff\].
- The Do-the-Work Guarantee in its exact wording when stated in full \[Handoff\].
- Strong, true lines stay strong. Don't hedge for imagined critics ("He saved 75" stays) \[Decisions, How Davon Works\].

**Claims we never make:**

- Guaranteed safety, a saved marriage, or a transformation \[Pressure Test\].
- Fake timers, made-up "value" numbers, invented testimonials or counts. The "14,000 men have found their type" line stays hidden until it's real \[Handoff\].
- Anything about a whole religion, ethnicity or nationality. Name the ideology, the organisation or the crime \[Strategy, Sentiment Research\].
- "Men are flooding back to church." Say "for the first time in Barna's tracking." Never cite the withdrawn UK "Quiet Revival" numbers \[VOC, Sentiment Research\].
- Anything assuming he has a wife or kids, unless his quiz answer says so \[Handoff\].
- A motive police haven't stated \[Viewer Filter\].
- Porn or lust content of any kind \[Decisions\].
- A sales line in the same breath as real victims \[Viewer Filter\].

**Flag:** the files disagree on naming politicians. The Show Bible says "Polarizing is good. Name politicians and public figures." The Sentiment Research says don't. VOC says it's allowed in organic posts, and that paid ads naming them need Meta's political ad authorization and a "Paid for by" disclaimer. Your call.

## 11. What you're likely to get wrong

1. **Writing for the dad and losing the 22-year-old.** The research was built for fathers 28 to 50. The audience is now 18 to 45, and the younger single man is a full core \[VOC update\]. Read every line twice: once as him, once as a dad of three. The merge fields only fix the funnel; videos and captions have no merge fields.
2. **Reaching for fear because it's what the older files say.** The 23 Oct work is built on threat ("the world is coming for your family," "the wolves"). Strategy v3 sells respect and reward. Fear can open a video. It must never close a sale \[Strategy, Decisions\].
3. **Pitching a man who is braced for the pitch.** He's heard "step up" for 20 years and is looking for a reason to call this a funnel \[VOC, Viewer Filter\]. The story has to land with the ask cut off. One fake fact or one cringe line and he screenshots it.
4. **Swagger instead of strength.** He's allergic to machismo, Driscoll-era posturing and the alpha crowd. Calm, cost and sacrifice win. Kolbe and Doss, the two biggest outliers, are men who gave themselves up, not men who won fights \[VOC, Strategy\].
5. **Assuming one tradition.** No denomination is named, but Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant men all watch and will check if you claim their saint or skip their history. Kolbe is Catholic, and the story should say so plainly \[Viewer Filter\].
6. **Overlooking the wife.** She shares, tags and buys gifts, and she's one of the four Viewer Filter readers \[VOC, Pressure Test\]. Nothing in the funnel speaks to her, and the files have no first-person wives' voice yet.
7. **Skipping his biggest private fight without noticing.** For the younger man that's lust \[VOC\]. You've ruled it out \[Decisions\]. Fine, but watch for DM replies and refund notes that say "this didn't touch my real problem."
8. **Asking for STRONG before the quiz exists.** "Unanswered comments on our best video are the worst outcome" \[Strategy\]. No ask goes out until the quiz, DM automation and email are built. If Stand Firm isn't ready, the offer must honestly say waitlist \[Viewer Filter\].
9. **Faking proof.** No testimonials, counters or "spots left" until they're real \[Handoff\]. The founding 50 must be a live count. This audience punishes a fake number harder than most.
10. **Thinking Instagram behaves like TikTok and YouTube.** Only Instagram turns a comment into a DM, and it allows one DM until he replies \[Comments and DMs\]. TikTok and YouTube viewers need the bio link and hand replies, and Shorts viewers mostly can't click. Long-form is the cheap email source \[Strategy\].
11. **Treating him as a buyer of information.** He doesn't believe another book or devotional will stick \[VOC, Pressure Test\]. What he's buying is a type that names his strength, and a box he can tick tonight. Sell the doing, not the content.
12. **Hedging strong lines.** You've said not to soften true lines for imagined critics \[Decisions, How Davon Works\]. The Viewer Filter still requires every claim to be no stronger than the source. Both rules hold at once: check the fact, then say it straight.
13. **Leaving out the outside enemies.** He feels the threat from outside as strongly as the failure inside, and the research says the real shape is both together: "outside threat plus inside failure, resolved through protecting and passing down" \[Sentiment Research\]. Name the jihadists, the ideology, the media and the policy plainly, by the platform rule in section 4 \[VOC, Strategy\].
14. **Following the 46-or-older answer.** Quiz Q8 includes "46 or older" though the ICP stops at 45 \[Handoff\]. Keep it (some older men will come), but don't let their answers steer the copy. Track buy rate by age and life stage, as Strategy asks.
