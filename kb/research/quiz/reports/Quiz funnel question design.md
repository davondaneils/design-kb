# Build Stand Firm's quiz in eleven taps

Stand Firm's quiz should have **eleven one-tap questions in about two to three minutes**. Four of them are forced-choice scenario items that score the Protector, Builder, Leader or Warrior type. The rest collect the data that changes something he will see: life stage, the area he wants stronger, his obstacle, the outside threat he fears, the cost of staying passive, when in the day he has time, and an age band. Two interstitials, a short loader that shows the real scoring work, and an email gate that names his type before asking for his address complete the flow. Cold Instagram traffic on phones finishes at the bottom of every vendor range: social and paid traffic completes at **35 to 60%**, against 70 to 85% for email subscribers ([Outgrow](https://outgrow.co/blog/quiz-engagement-benchmarks-completion-rates)). So the 30-question Liven model and MadMuscles' 35 steps are the wrong length for this audience, even though their stage logic is worth copying. Davon's decision of 2026-10-29 lets the quiz sell with pain, the outside threat and the cost of passivity, and a follow-up edit asks for every line at full strength. The evidence agrees, with one condition. Framing effects are small either way. Pain works hardest when he states it in his own answers mid-quiz and the result then turns it into the prescription. The binding rules still hold: the result must change with his answers, there is no fake proof, counts or timers, ideologies and crimes are named but never a people group, and no banned words appear. The recommended screen-by-screen set is at the end.

## Eleven questions suit cold phone traffic, not thirty

The length evidence agrees on direction even though none of it is controlled. Outgrow reports **65 to 85% completion for 3 to 7 questions, 45 to 65% for 8 to 15, and 25 to 45% for 16 or more**, and puts personality quizzes at the top of its type ranges (60 to 80%) ([Outgrow](https://outgrow.co/blog/quiz-engagement-benchmarks-completion-rates)). Interact's portfolio averages **65% start-to-finish and 40.1% start-to-lead**, with coaching and courses at 59.1% completion and 44.9% lead capture ([Interact](https://www.tryinteract.com/blog/quiz-conversion-rate-report/)). Interact's help centre puts the ideal at 13 questions for straight logic and 7 for branching ([Interact Help](http://help.tryinteract.com/en/articles/10752954-how-many-questions-should-my-quiz-have-to-maximize-conversions)). In RevenueHunt's Shopify data, revenue per completion peaks at a median of 5 to 7 questions while raw conversion peaks at 9 to 12 ([RevenueHunt](https://revenuehunt.com/quiz-creation-mistakes-that-hurt-your-ecommerce-sales/)). An independent corpus of 119 quiz apps found a mean of 3.9 question screens and a 90th percentile of 10, and recommends mapping "every question to the downstream personalization it drives" ([Lazyweb](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/how-many-quiz-questions-onboarding)). The long funnels that work (Noom at up to 113 screens) work because "every question clearly builds toward a payoff" and their offers carry annual subscription value ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel)). One practitioner suggests 5 to 8 questions for one-time, low-LTV purchases ([RocketShip HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/noom-quiz-paywall-funnel-replication)). A one-time $27 program sits in that low band.

Eleven questions sit inside Outgrow's 8-to-15 band and above Interact's branching optimum. They earn that length only if each one passes the decision rule the sources share: **a question stays only if its answer changes the result, the offer or the email track** ([Octane AI](https://www.octaneai.com/blog/quiz-questions-that-convert)). Every question in the recommended set passes. The two weakest (time of day and age band) are the first to cut if they leak.

### Order: on-promise identity first, pain in the middle, easy items last

Survey methodology and the commercial exemplars agree. Breakoff comes from threat, effort and irrelevance, not from position as such. A Harvard randomised test found **no attrition penalty for putting demographics first** ([Shift Project](https://shift.hks.harvard.edu/dont-save-the-demographics-for-last/)). There is also limited evidence that salient, interesting topics first reduce breakoff ([SMIF 2024](https://surveyinsights.org/?p=19071)). Sensitive items belong **halfway to two-thirds of the way through**, and a survey should not end on one ([Duke tipsheet](https://dism.duke.edu/files/2020/05/Tipsheet-Sensitive_Questions.pdf)). Davon's two pasted DTC Quizbuilder posts disagree on whether Q1 should be age or goal ([Davon's DTC note](../research_notes/Quiz%20funnel%20question%20design/dtc-quizbuilder-2m-in-90-days.md)). For a type quiz promising "Find your strength, and what's holding it back," the answer is an identity question that already scores his type: it is on-promise, fast and self-expressive. Self-expressive matters because foot-in-the-door works through self-perception, and its pooled effect is only about **r = .10** ([Burger 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15661679/); [meta-analysis summary](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1162908816300470)). Q1's real job is to cut perceived effort, not to lock him in. Age moves to the end, where it is an easy last tap, because it changes no copy (brief item 14 says not to let 46-plus answers steer copy).

**Planning math (inference, not a promise):** 1,000 landers × 50% start × 55% completion is about 275 finishers. About 60% opt in, giving roughly 165 leads. At RevenueHunt's 5 to 9% completer purchase rate, that is 14 to 25 orders at $27. Email then carries the late fifth of buyers. Instagram's in-app browser is the default context. ## Forced-choice scenarios score the type and resist the flattering answer

The type items should be **single-tap, 3 to 5 options, each option mapped to one type and equally attractive**. That makes them a light quasi-ipsative forced choice, the format the faking literature favours. Forced-choice measures show score inflation of only **0.06** between low and high stakes when blocks are balanced for desirability ([Cao and Drasgow 2019](https://psycnet.apa.org/doi/10.1037/apl0000414)), and quasi-ipsative forced choice resists faking best ([Martinez and Salgado 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34659043)). For Stand Firm this is not about catching liars. It stops every man tapping the "ideal" option, which would collapse the four types into one. Levesque warns that a lopsided bucket split signals "collapsed" buckets ([Wedmore](https://www.jameswedmoretraining.com/blog/how-i-doubled-sales-with-the-ask-method)). The working rule (inference): **if any type exceeds about 40% of the first 500 completers, rewrite the options before scaling.**

Likert grids are the wrong tool for the type items, because grid satisfaction drops ([Revilla et al. 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0736585316300776)) and an agree scale never makes him choose who he is. The Liven agreement barrage has a place, but as single "that's me" statements, not a grid ([Davon's Liven note](../research_notes/Quiz%20funnel%20question%20design/overthinking-quiz-liven-clone-breakdown.md)). Images help when they are the answer tiles ([Couper et al. 2004](https://surveyinsights.org/?p=4350&print=pdf)) and hurt when he has to produce something ([Bosch, Revilla et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36247522)). Fast mobile respondents show primacy effects ([Malhotra 2008](https://academic.oup.com/poq/article/72/5/914/1832496)), so rotate option order on the type items. The Tai Chi breakdown's rule, **never the same question type more than twice in a row** ([@DTC_Quizbuilder](https://x.com/DTC_Quizbuilder/status/1987552724800483393)), is untested but cheap: alternate image tiles, text scenarios, quote cards and a multi-select. Keep options to **4 plus a "none" escape**. The DTC Quizbuilder's own fix of a long past-solutions question cut its drop-off from 22% to 9% that way ([Davon's DTC note](../research_notes/Quiz%20funnel%20question%20design/dtc-quizbuilder-2m-in-90-days.md), self-reported). Single-choice taps should auto-advance with no delay. MadMuscles measured 45 to 72 ms from tap to next screen, with a visible back button from step 1 ([MadMuscles live funnel](https://madmuscles.com/en/funnel/default-uni-soft/step-one)).

## Pain now earns a place mid-quiz, then hands off to the reward

Several research notes were written under the old "sell the reward, never the pain" rule and recommend gain-only questions. **Davon lifted that rule for the quiz on 2026-10-29**, then added that lines should be written "at full strength" as long as the facts are true ([messaging brief update](../research_notes/Quiz%20funnel%20question%20design/messaging-brief-update-2026-10-28-outside-enemies.md)). This report follows that decision. The evidence does not argue against it, but it does shape how to do it.

Framing effects on their own are tiny. Across 93 studies, gain frames beat loss frames for prevention messages by **r = .03** ([O'Keefe and Jensen 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17934940)). Loss frames won slightly for detecting a problem, **r = -.04** ([O'Keefe and Jensen 2009](https://academic.oup.com/joc/article-abstract/59/2/296/4098529)). Gain framing won for actual physical-activity behaviour at **r = .083** ([Gallagher and Updegraff 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21993844/)). The larger effect is on resistance: gain-framed narratives draw **much less counter-arguing (d = -0.57)** and more self-efficacy ([Kim and Chen 2025](https://academic.oup.com/joc/article-abstract/75/3/220/8011622)). Counter-arguing is this audience's reflex, because the brief describes a man braced for the pitch. Regulatory fit adds a twist: loss frames persuade more when the goal is prevention, and gain frames when it is promotion ([Lee and Aaker 2004](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2004-10747-001)). Protecting the people he loves from an outside threat is a prevention goal. Building and leading are promotion goals.

Three design rules follow (inference). First, **make the pain his own answer, not our assertion.** The DTC "users diagnose themselves" principle ([@DTC_Quizbuilder](https://x.com/DTC_Quizbuilder/status/1987552724800483393)) and Liven's self-label, deepen and cost stages ([Davon's Liven note](../research_notes/Quiz%20funnel%20question%20design/overthinking-quiz-liven-clone-breakdown.md)) both rely on this. He cannot argue with a cost he picked himself. Second, **put the pain cluster at the 60 to 80% mark** (obstacle, outside threat, cost of passivity). Follow it with a hope interstitial, then end on easy taps before the gate. That matches Duke's placement advice and Levesque's "Killer" framework, which runs on loss aversion ([Quiz Funnel Blueprint](https://quiz-funnel-masterclass.s3.amazonaws.com/ASK_QuizFunnel_Blueprint.pdf)). Third, **frame the result's stakes by regulatory fit.** Protector and Warrior results lead with what is at risk. Builder and Leader results lead with what gets built. Every result closes on the prescription. Asking about intentions nudges behaviour, with a small effect (**d = 0.24** across 116 tests) that is largest for desirable, easy actions ([Wood et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4931712/)). That supports ending on a concrete start-day commitment.

Binding limits on pain copy: name the ideology, organisation or crime and never a people group; no porn or lust content; no claim stronger than its source; no spoken flag words; and no assumption of a wife or kids unless his life-stage answer says so. Copy below is written at full strength within those limits.

## Two axes: the type flatters, the obstacle routes the offer

Levesque's system runs in two steps. A deep-dive survey built on the "single most important question" ("What is your single biggest challenge...") finds **3 to 5 buckets**. A micro-commitment quiz then sorts each taker into one and "prescribes" the matching offer ([Mottola notes on Ask](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/56b8be1227d4bda9ae42b4ca/t/579a9b602e69cf766304bffa/1469750113717/Ryan+Levesque,+ASK.pdf); [Ask Method Blueprint](https://s3.amazonaws.com/askmmc-19/AskMethodBlueprint.pdf)). He separates primary from secondary segmentation and maps outcomes from several questions. He says to start "1:1:1", with one source, one path and one offer, and reposition that single offer per bucket ([askmethod.com](https://askmethod.com/why-ask-method)). That fits Stand Firm exactly. **The type is the result label that makes him feel seen. The obstacle and the area he wants stronger are the buckets that route the angle of the one $27 offer and the email track.** Keep the two axes separate, so a Protector held back by his phone hears a different pitch from a Protector who says nobody showed him how. Levesque's claim that segmentation can "2-3X" conversion is self-reported ([Quiz Funnel Blueprint](https://quiz-funnel-masterclass.s3.amazonaws.com/ASK_QuizFunnel_Blueprint.pdf)). McKinsey's **10 to 15% revenue lift** (range 5 to 25%) is the sounder prior ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/the-value-of-getting-personalization-right-or-wrong-is-multiplying)). Levesque's closest case is a Christian-singles "#1 reason" quiz that claimed 10,000 leads in 14 days, also self-reported ([Quiz Funnel Blueprint](https://quiz-funnel-masterclass.s3.amazonaws.com/ASK_QuizFunnel_Blueprint.pdf)). One gap remains: Stand Firm has no deep-dive survey yet. Until it does, the option wording should come from the VOC phrases in the brief ("phone before prayer", "nobody showed me how"), and the open-ended SMIQ should run once in the first email to refine the buckets.

## Accurate-feeling results are built from his own answers

People rated a generic sketch at **4.26 out of 5** for accuracy when they believed it came from their own test ([Forer effect summary](https://atticusli.com/replication-crisis/forer-barnum-effect)). Acceptance rises with favourable content, with belief that the result came from one's own answers, and with the source's authority ([Dickson and Kelly 1985](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2466/pr0.1985.57.2.367)). People also want feedback that confirms how they already see themselves ([Swann](https://sk.sagepub.com/hnbk/edvol/hdbk_socialpsychtheories2/chpt/selfverification-theory)). The honest way to use those forces is to make the content true and the derivation visible. BuzzFeed writes the results first so that "the answers genuinely correspond to the results" ([HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/buzzfeed-quiz-how-do-they-work_n_4810992)). In a controlled test, mirroring the visitor's own words in a headline lifted signups **31.4%** ([Unbounce / ConversionLab](https://unbounce.com/landing-page-examples/built-using-unbounce/dynamic-text-a-b-test)).

So the result page should (1) name him with a noun, "You're a Protector." Noun labels moved validated turnout **10.9 points** over verb wording ([Bryan et al. 2011](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/59faaf5b01b9500001e95457/5bc55edda8a9d806acce2dbd_Bryan%2C%20C.%20J.%2C%20Walton%2C%20G.%20M.%2C%20Rogers%2C%20T.%2C%20%26%20Dweck%2C%20C.%20S.%202011.pdf)). (2) Show a secondary edge ("with a Builder streak"), so the label opens a door instead of boxing him in ([JSTOR Daily](https://daily.jstor.org/what-do-personality-quizzes-really-tell-you)). (3) Run a "Because you said" block that quotes three of his answers verbatim. (4) Give a two-sided shadow, since positive statements offset by a credible negative are accepted most readily ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/science/Barnum-Effect)). (5) Echo his obstacle and his cost as the stakes. (6) Prescribe the 30 days as the cure for exactly that profile, which is the Ask "Band-aid to Cure" move ([Quiz Funnel Blueprint](https://quiz-funnel-masterclass.s3.amazonaws.com/ASK_QuizFunnel_Blueprint.pdf)). The line Stand Firm must not cross is the one that sank credibility elsewhere. The FTC's case against MadMuscles' parent alleges deceptive subscription funnels ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-sues-stop-sprawling-enterprise-operating-unlawful-subscription-schemes)), and a legal teardown names "a score that's the same no matter what you enter" as a manufactured diagnosis ([Applica](https://applica.agency/webinars/subscriptions-without-lawsuits-a-legal-growth-teardown-of-app-subscription-funnels)). The RealFaith "Which Is The Real Man?" quiz gives every taker the same result ([realfaith.com/man](https://realfaith.com/man)). Stand Firm's result must change with every scored answer, and retaking the quiz with different answers must visibly produce a different type.

## Progress, interstitials and the loader must tell the truth

A 32-experiment meta-analysis found that a constant-speed progress bar does **not** reliably cut breakoff (OR about 1.07, not significant). A bar that is slow early and fast late **raises** breakoff (OR about 1.56). Fast-then-slow lowers it (OR about 0.80) ([Villar et al. 2013](https://core.ac.uk/download/42630452.pdf)). Intermittent feedback softens slow progress ([Conrad et al. 2010](https://faculty.isr.umich.edu/fredconrad/?p=142)). Outgrow's claimed 12 to 18% lift from progress bars has no stated method ([Outgrow](https://outgrow.co/blog/quiz-engagement-benchmarks-completion-rates)). The design that follows: show an honest **"3 of 11" step count** from the first screen, count only questions (MadMuscles counts interstitials and the loader), make the first four taps feel instant, and **keep the counter visible at the name and email screens**. MadMuscles hides it exactly there ([MadMuscles live funnel](https://madmuscles.com/en/funnel/default-uni-soft/step-one)).

Interstitials should appear every 4 to 6 questions, with one job each. Noom runs a motivational page every 2 to 3 questions ([Growth Models](https://growthmodels.co/noom-marketing/)), and MadMuscles about every 10 steps. An 11-question quiz needs only two. The first comes after the type cluster: a halfway card with a Scripture line and a true partial readout ("Your answers so far lean Protector"). That is intermittent feedback and a self-verification hook in one. The second comes after the pain cluster: the hope turn. Liven's version uses an unsourced "87% of overthinkers" stat ([Davon's Liven note](../research_notes/Quiz%20funnel%20question%20design/overthinking-quiz-liven-clone-breakdown.md)), which is banned here. Stand Firm's version uses the brief's own line, "Nobody taught you. That's on them. Staying passive is on you." Neither card may carry a count or testimonial that is not real.

The loader rests on the labor illusion. In those experiments a transparent 30-second wait was chosen over an instant result **62% of the time, against 42% for a blind progress bar**. The effect reversed when the outcome was unfavourable ([Buell and Norton 2011](https://quartz-digitalbrain.pages.dev/The-labor-illusion.pdf)). A type result is favourable by design, so the loader fits. Keep it to **5 to 8 seconds**, well under the tested waits, with captions that describe the real computation. The DTC Quizbuilder's 10-to-15-second loader with a big before/after testimonial ([Davon's DTC note](../research_notes/Quiz%20funnel%20question%20design/dtc-quizbuilder-2m-in-90-days.md)) is out until real testimonials exist. Embed the start-day question inside it, as MadMuscles does with "Are you ready to make the commitment?" Levesque reports that a simple choice just before the email ask builds "action taking momentum" and can raise opt-in ([Roger Dooley](https://www.rogerdooley.com/ryan-levesque-choose)).

## The email gate belongs after the loader, with his type already named

RevenueHunt's data shows the trade-off. A hard gate captures nearly every finisher but loses completion: **76% against 85% for an optional ask**. The optional ask also led on revenue per completion, **$7.77 against $6.06**. No ask finished highest (90%) and earned least ($3.99) ([RevenueHunt](https://revenuehunt.com/quiz-creation-mistakes-that-hurt-your-ecommerce-sales/)). The email step is "the single biggest leak" ([RevenueHunt docs](https://docs.revenuehunt.com/customer-success/reduce-dropoff)). Stand Firm is lead-gen first: a fifth of quiz orders arrive after 30 days, and welcome flows earn far more per recipient than campaigns ([Klaviyo](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/revenue-per-recipient)). So **gate the full result, but name his type on the gate screen**, as the practitioner preview-before-gate advice suggests ([GrowthLens](https://www.growthlens.io/blog/quiz-funnel-completion-rate-optimization)). Then run a test cell with a small "Show me now, skip email" link once each arm has a few hundred starts ([RevenueHunt docs](https://docs.revenuehunt.com/customer-success/reduce-dropoff/)). Place the gate after the loader, so "your profile is ready" is true when he reads it. MadMuscles shows "Your workout plan is ready!" before the inputs that build the plan are collected.

Gate copy should be one field, email only ([SegMetrics](https://segmetrics.io/guides/download/Playbook-Optin-Funnel-Quiz.pdf)). The CTA names the payoff, as in Noom's "See my results" ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel)), and a specific trust line answers the fact that only **33% of consumers trust companies with their data** ([Qualtrics XM Institute](https://www.qualtrics.com/m/www.xminstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/XMI_RR-DS_ConsumerPreferencePrivacyPersonalization-2025.pdf)). Proposed copy: headline "**{Name}, you're a Protector.**" Body: "Your full profile is ready: what makes you strong, what's been holding you back, and the Day 1 move built for it. Where should it go?" Button: "**Send my Protector profile**". Trust line: "Your answers stay with us. We never sell them. No spam, leave in one tap." Use an email-type input and keep the button in the thumb zone.

## Conclusion

The craft question for Stand Firm turns on sequence more than on length or framing. A man braced for a pitch accepts pain he has named himself. He believes a type he can see was derived from his own taps. He gives his email more readily when his type is already on the screen and the profile is truly ready. Davon's lift on pain does not change the shape of the quiz. It adds a middle movement (obstacle, threat, cost) that the reward-only drafts left out, and regulatory fit indicates where that movement should hit hardest: Protector and Warrior results.

None of the benchmarks will transfer cleanly. They are ecommerce, vendor-reported and blind to faith audiences, so the first 500 completers are the real research. The numbers to watch are per-question drop-off above 10%, any type above 40%, and buy rate split by type, obstacle, life stage and source video. The obstacle bucket, not the type, is the likeliest predictor of a $27 purchase, and no published source has tested it. That makes it the first finding this funnel should produce for itself.
