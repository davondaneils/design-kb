# Research: 02 Proof and trust design

Researcher, 2026-10-10. Method as in note 01.

## Key findings
1. Reviews move purchases, most for expensive items: five reviews lift purchase likelihood 270% over none; 380% for higher-priced products, 190% for lower-priced (C20). Medill Spiegel Research Center, first published 2017, page dated 2025.
2. Perfect scores backfire: purchase likelihood peaks at 4.0 to 4.7 and falls toward 5.0, "too good to be true" (C20).
3. Count beats score: nearly twice as many respondents chose 4.5 stars from 57 ratings over 5 stars from 4 (C22). An average without a count is distrusted (C22).
4. People look for the bad news: 53% of test users actively sought negative reviews; Baymard recommends a distribution summary as horizontal bars that act as filters, hidden at 5 or fewer reviews (C21).
5. Trust on the web comes from design quality, up-front disclosure, comprehensive current content and connection to the rest of the web (C19). Up-front disclosure is the conversion lever most premium sites under-use (opinion).
6. Payment fields feel less safe than the rest of the page even when they are not; visually encapsulate them (border, background, shading) and add a seal or lock icon; a homemade seal beat most SSL vendor seals except Norton (C23). Baymard's own users: 19% abandoned because they did not trust the site with card details (C5).
7. Legal floor: FTC final rule (Aug 14, 2024) bans fake or AI-invented reviews, review suppression by threats, undisclosed insider reviews, fake "independent" review sites and fake social indicators (C24).
8. Social proof is not automatically good: social features can distract from calls to action; test before assuming (C?, NN/g social proof, C50).

## Numbers
- 270% / 380% / 190% (C20). Peak 4.0 to 4.7 (C20).
- 53% seek negatives; 43% of top sites lack a distribution (C21).
- 19% card-trust abandonment (C5).

## Conflicting views
- Trust seals: Baymard finds seals help at payment (C23). No source here supports seals in a hero or nav; the claim that they "look cheap" there is opinion (exemplars Stripe, Linear, Apple show none on marketing pages; Vercel shows compliance badges on its pricing page, C46, which is a B2B security buyer's question, not decoration).
