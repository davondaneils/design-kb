# Research 05: Strategy to visual identity

Researcher, 2026-10-13. Brief: `kb/briefs/strategy/05-strategy-to-visual-identity.md`.

## Key findings
1. **Distinctive assets are measured on fame and uniqueness.** Ehrenberg-Bass: distinctive assets "are non-brand name elements, such as colours, logos or characters that uniquely signal the brand" [E1]. The grid's quadrants: Use or Lose (famous and unique), Invest (unique, not famous), Avoid (famous, not unique; "such elements will trigger competitors to copy"), Test or ignore. "All new assets start with low uniqueness and fame." [E1]
2. **Benchmark (International Journal of Advertising, 2026; 1,162 assets, 21 categories, 4 countries, 9 years, 12,249 respondents):** shape-based assets (logos, packaging) average 40 percent fame and 71 percent uniqueness; color assets 12 percent fame and 39 percent uniqueness, the weakest type [E2]. The average asset: 26 percent fame, 54 percent uniqueness; quadrants split at 50 percent [E2]. "Reliance on color alone as a Distinctive Asset, therefore, carries substantial risk." [E2] Color also has the highest competitive intensity (HHI 0.31); characters, logos and fonts the lowest (0.69, 0.61, 0.60) [E2, citing Ward et al. 2020]. Strong case: the GEICO Gecko, 87 percent fame and 98 percent uniqueness (Romaniuk 2018, cited in E2).
3. **Test assets by recognition, not liking.** The same paper: "packaging redesigns based on focus groups and brand attitude measures has been associated with less successful outcomes, whereas approaches grounded in Distinctive Asset research are linked to more successful outcomes (Caruso et al. 2025)" [E2].
4. **Attention-grabbing creative needs stronger branding.** Romaniuk: "The more attention-grabbing the creative, the harder the branding has to work to succeed" [E3].
5. **Shape carries attributes, weakly, and words override it.** Jiang, Gorn, Galli and Chattopadhyay (2016, Journal of Consumer Research 42(5)), five experiments: "circular- versus angular-logo shapes activate softness and hardness associations", which shift judgments such as a shoe's comfort or durability; but "There are no logo shape effects on attribute judgments ... when the headline of the ad highlights a product attribute that differs from the inference drawn from the logo shape" [E4].
6. **Sound and shape map across cultures.** Ćwiek et al. (2022, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B): bouba/kiki tested in 25 languages, 9 language families, 10 writing systems; "strong evidence for the effect across languages" [E5]. Supports rounded or angular letterforms and marks as a cross-cultural cue (R, our inference for brand use).
7. **Typeface persona is perceived** (Brumberger 2003) [D5 in research 04].
8. **Asset equity is real and can be lost.** Tropicana 2009: PepsiCo scrapped a January carton redesign within weeks and brought back the old pack and "the longtime Tropicana brand symbol, an orange from which a straw protrudes" (NYT) [E6]. A 20 percent unit sales drop is widely reported from Ad Age citing Nielsen; the original Ad Age article was not fetched (tier 5 relays only) [E7].
9. **Earned fame permits simplification.** Mastercard 2019 dropped its name from the symbol "with more than 80 percent of people spontaneously recognizing the Mastercard Symbol without the word 'mastercard'" [E8].
10. **Accidental assets count.** Monzo's coral card "was more of a happy accident than a marketing masterstroke" (WIRED) [E9]; the 2022 refresh "audited our visual brand codes" and kept coral as "what helps keep us distinctive"; it broke category imagery codes on purpose: "The banking market is flooded with generic people photography. So we needed a style that was unique to Monzo." [E10]
11. **A treatment can be the asset**: COLLINS' Spotify duotone (Design Week, via art direction research C7) [E11].
12. **Why distinctive, emotional brand work matters (budget context).** Binet and Field, IPA databank (996 campaigns, 700 brands, 83 categories [E12]): chart titles "The 60:40 split delivers maximum effectiveness" and "Emotional campaigns yield stronger long-term business effects" (The Long and the Short of It, figures 38, 44, 53, 54; text extraction of Tom Roach's compilation, PDF not parseable here) [E13]. For B2B (2019, LinkedIn B2B Institute) "Activation ... should get 54% of spend while brand building should receive 46%" (The Drum) [E14]. These are databank averages, not laws (kb/references/strategy-brand.md).
13. **Color evidence is weak and conventions are not causes** (design-color psychology-brand: Elliot 2015; Labrecque and Milne 2012 as "a weak nudge") [E15].

## Conflicting views
- Hue psychology claims versus evidence: see design-color psychology-brand (folklore list).
- Liking versus recognition as the test of a new identity [E2]: practice still runs preference focus groups; the evidence favors measuring fame and uniqueness.
