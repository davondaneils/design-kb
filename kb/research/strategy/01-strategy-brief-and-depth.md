# Research 01: The strategy brief and how deep to go

Researcher, 2026-10-13. Brief: `kb/briefs/strategy/01-strategy-brief-and-depth.md`. Fetched with Parallel Search and WebFetch on 2026-10-13 unless marked. Status column: C consensus, R research, P practitioner, X contested. Revised 2026-10-13: rows A10 to A16 added for brand architecture and naming (v0.1.1 gap), and the brief-length decision.

## Key findings
1. **The full brand process has five phases**: conducting research, clarifying strategy, designing identity, creating touchpoints, managing assets (Wheeler, every edition since the third; table of contents of the third edition and a Wiley-published summary) [A1][A2]. Strategy is phase 2 and is short relative to the whole: in the third edition table of contents it spans pages 116 to 122 of a 90 to 178 page process section [A1]. Opinion: strategy is a narrow, decisive step, not a document genre.
2. **Wheeler's strategy phase is about narrowing.** AIGA Raleigh's recap of her talk: "Narrow the focus. Brands get stronger as you narrow the focus." and the phase ends in "a brand brief" [A3].
3. **The design system already demands a short strategy for small jobs.** design-playbook-brand section 0: for a campaign, event or one-off artifact, "write three lines instead (audience, the one true fact the idea comes from, the competitor wall you checked)" [A4]. The held-out test showed the cost of over-scoping: the playbook "demands website-strategy and a full guidelines page before the mark; on a poster-plus-site brief that is scope the brief did not ask for" [A5].
4. **website-strategy is built for a client proposal.** Its own description: "End-to-end website strategy framework for freelance proposals and client projects" with six stages ending in "proposal/build brief" [A6]. Its stage 5 says "A designer or developer reading it should be able to make every decision without asking a question" [A6]. That goal is shared; the output length is not.
5. **Each downstream skill already asks for a strategy input**: art direction needs a true fact ("Positioning comes from website-strategy") [A7]; design-color needs the competitor set [A8]; design-copy 03 takes "brand positioning and personality inputs" from website-strategy [A9]. A single brief removes three separate asks.
6. **Brand architecture is a spectrum, not a binary.** Aaker and Joachimsthaler (2000) place every offer on a "brand relationship spectrum" from **house of brands** (not connected, shadow endorser) through **endorsed brands** (token, linked name, strong endorsement) and **subbrands** (co-drivers, master brand as driver) to the **branded house** (same identity, different identity) [A10]. (Corrected 2026-10-13, second critic pass: "master brand as driver" is a subbrand position in the article's Figure 1, not the branded house.) Subbrands and endorsements "allow brands to stretch across products and markets", "protect brands from being diluted by over-stretching", and "signal that an offering is new and different" [A10]. Aaker's decision questions for a new offer: "Will existing brands enhance the new offering? Will the new offering enhance an existing brand? Is there a compelling reason to generate a new brand?"; "A sub-brand will allow some distance from a master brand, an endorsed brand more, and a new brand the most" [A11].
7. **Distinctive assets run through the architecture.** Romaniuk's book has a chapter on extending assets across sub-brands with the section "Protect the parent brand as a priority" [A12]. Ward et al. (2025): products in visually cohesive portfolios are matched to their portfolio by 79 versus 35 percent of respondents and recalled 17 percent more often (research 05, E22).
8. **Naming: legal strength runs opposite to descriptiveness.** USPTO: "Strong trademarks are suggestive, fanciful, or arbitrary. Weak trademarks are descriptive or generic." Weak marks (descriptive or generic) "are hard to protect against competitors and often are not federally registrable"; descriptive marks are registrable "only" in certain circumstances, such as acquired distinctiveness [A13]. Registration fails on "likelihood of confusion" when marks are similar and goods related; search before filing [A14]. Bali et al. (2024): suggestive distinctive assets show lower fame and uniqueness than non-suggestive ones (research 05, E18); whether the same holds for names was not checked. Naming method (generation, linguistic and cultural screening, full clearance) is out of this skill's depth: route to a naming specialist and trademark counsel (opinion).
9. **Brief length.** The research suggested 400 to 600 words (opinion, finding 1); the critic measured 680 words at about 1.3 A4 sides at 10pt. v0.1.1 sets 600 as the warn line (see Numbers).

## Numbers
- Depth tiers (opinion, from A4 and A5): inherit (0 new lines), three lines, one page (about 400 to 600 words, opinion; v0.1.1 lints at 600 warn, 800 fail), full (website-strategy stages 2 to 5 as inputs, then the one-page brief as the summary).
- Wheeler phases: 5.

## Conflicting views
- website-strategy says "Work through the stages in order. Each one feeds the next" [A6]; design-playbook-brand section 0 says strategy depth follows the brief [A4]. Resolution (opinion): run all stages only for full-depth jobs; for others, take the fields the brief needs.
