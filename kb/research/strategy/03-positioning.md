# Research 03: Positioning

Researcher, 2026-10-13. Brief: `kb/briefs/strategy/03-positioning.md`. Revised 2026-10-13: rows C17 to C25 added for facts the critic put in the skill (no-decision rate, Dunford's definition, Neumeier) and for the v0.1.1 gaps (trends, sales-pitch order, positioning styles).

## Key findings
1. **Five components** (Dunford): "Competitive Alternatives, Differentiated 'Features' or 'Capabilities', Value for customers, Target Customer Segmentation, Market Category" [C1]. The book page adds "How to leverage market trends" as a later lesson [C2].
2. **Order matters, alternatives first.** "We start with competitive alternatives, or what would customers do if our solution didn't exist." [C3] Each component depends on the others: features are "only 'differentiated' when you compare them to competitive alternatives" [C3].
3. **The status quo is the first competitor.** "the first competitive alternative we need to beat is the status quo" [C4]. And do not list every possible competitor: "Positioning shouldn't involve a test of your internet research skills" [C4].
4. **The fill-in positioning statement is a trap as a method.** Dunford calls it "not only pointless but potentially dangerous" because "the exercise assumes that there is only one answer for each of the blanks" [C5]. website-strategy stage 4 uses a Dunford-adapted statement as the output of the stage [C6]; the two agree if the statement is written last.
5. **Market category is a frame chosen for advantage**: "your best market category is the context you position your product in such that your unique value is obvious to your target customers" [C3]. Positioning "is firmly rooted in the present" [C7].
6. **Onlyness** (Neumeier, Zag): "Our brand is the only ___ that ___" [C8]; "if you can't say why your brand is both different and compelling in a few words, don't fix your positioning statement...fix your company" [C9]. The swap test is a practitioner addition: "put a competitor's name in place and see if it still fits" (Dan Mall, citing Zag's what/how/who/where/why/when format) [C10].
7. **Perceived differentiation is low in real markets.** Romaniuk, Sharp and Ehrenberg (2007, Australasian Marketing Journal), data across many categories and two countries: "there is a low level of perceived differentiation across competing brands", and they propose "to instead place distinctiveness at the centre of brand strategy" [C11]. This is contested by the positioning school (Dunford, Neumeier, Ries) [C1][C8]; the two address different buyers (opinion, see Conflicting views).
8. **Public "only", "first", "best" claims need evidence.** FTC: advertisers must "have a reasonable basis for advertising claims before they are disseminated"; express claims like "tests prove" need "at least the advertised level of substantiation" [C12]. UK CAP Code rule 3.7: marketers "must hold documentary evidence to prove claims that consumers are likely to regard as objective" [C13]. The ASA treated "the perfect network" as objective in context; obvious puffery is allowed under rule 3.2 [C14].
9. **One true fact, Schwartz's product analysis**: website-strategy stage 2 asks "What is the mechanism ... What proof exists that the mechanism works?" [C15]. The art direction skill builds its concept from "a true, specific and drawable" fact [C16].
10. **Status quo by the number.** "In enterprise software, we typically lose between 20% and 30% of deals to 'no decision'" (Dunford, linking a Strategic Dynamics figure) [C17]: about one in four, not most.
11. **Dunford's definition**: "Positioning defines how your product is a leader at delivering something that a well-defined set of customers cares a lot about." [C18] Her homepage uses a shorter line, "how our product is different and better than alternatives for a particular set of customers" [C24]; quote the article version.
12. **Neumeier**: "Remember, you can't advertise your way to onlyness" [C19]; the sentence continues "you have to start with it" after a dash, which the skill drops (house rule).
13. **Trends are an optional last layer.** Obviously Awesome teaches "How to leverage market trends to help buyers understand why making a purchase is important right now" (publisher description) [C20]; in her process the step is "Layer on a trend (but be careful)" (tier 5 summary) [C21]; her podcast: trends "don't always align with your product (and why you shouldn't force them to)", with "Long Island Blockchain" as the cautionary case [C22]. The book page also lists "three styles of positioning" [C2]; their names were not confirmed in a primary source this round, so the skill does not teach them.
14. **Pitch order follows positioning.** Sales Pitch (2023), in Dunford's words: "The pitch structure is composed of two main parts: the setup and the follow-through"; "The setup comprises three components: insight, alternatives, and 'the perfect world'"; the follow-through is differentiated value and how it is delivered, then proof [C23]. The insight answers "what does a customer need to know in order to understand why our differentiated value is important?" (Help Scout: "Customer service is a growth driver, not a cost center.") [C23]. Opinion: this is the section order for a positioning-led landing page (insight hero, alternatives, perfect world, product as the answer, proof, objections, ask). Positioning also stays present tense: "the best in the world at delivering something against the competitors you have right now" (mtpcon 2026 recap) [C25].

## Numbers
- Components: 5 (Dunford) [C1]. Swap test against 3 named competitors (opinion).
- Onlyness format: what, how, who, where, why, when [C10].

## Conflicting views
- **Differentiation (Dunford, Neumeier) versus distinctiveness (Sharp, Romaniuk)** [X]. Our resolution (opinion): positioning decides what the brand says to the in-market buyer comparing a shortlist (Dunford's B2B context: "your customer is coming to you with a shortlist of competitors" [C7]); distinctiveness decides what the brand looks like so the 95 percent out of market remember it (research 02, B6). Both rows go in the brief. The visual identity is judged on distinctiveness; the words carry the differentiation.
