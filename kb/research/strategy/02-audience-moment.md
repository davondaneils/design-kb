# Research 02: Audience as a person in a moment

Researcher, 2026-10-13. Brief: `kb/briefs/strategy/02-audience-moment.md`.

## Key findings
1. **Category entry points (CEPs) describe the buyer's situation, not the brand.** Romaniuk: CEPs "are the cues that category buyers use to access their memories when faced with a buying situation" and include internal cues (motives, emotions) and external cues (location, time of day) [B1]. "Category entry points are not about the brand, they're about the buyer" [B2]. Brand growth means being linked to many relevant CEPs: "we want to be behind as many of those doors as possible" [B2].
2. **Four forces decide a switch** (Moesta and Spiek, Re-Wired Group): "Two forces drive change: the push of the situation and the pull of a new solution. Two hold it back: the anxiety of the new and the habit of the present." [B3] "No push, no switch." [B3] JTBD was developed by Moesta "alongside Harvard Business School professor Clayton Christensen" [B4].
3. **Reducing anxiety often beats adding pull.** "More features can increase anxiety." and "Money-back guarantees, no-questions-asked returns, free trials, expert opinions ... worked by removing anxiety rather than adding pull." [B5]
4. **Most of the audience is not buying now.** Dawes (Ehrenberg-Bass, for the LinkedIn B2B Institute, May 2021): corporations change providers "around once every five years on average. That means only 20% of business buyers are 'in the market' over the course of an entire year; something like 5% in a quarter" [B6]. His caveat: "The 95% figure is not meant to be a precise rule. We're using it as a heuristic" [B6b]. He gives the method for any category: from a two-year interpurchase time, "50% are in the market over the course of a (whole) year, or around 13% in any quarter" [B6b]. (Corrected 2026-10-13: the earlier quote, attributed to Marketing Week, could not be found at any reachable URL; these are the paper's own words on the Institute's site.) Implication (opinion): most people who see a page or brand asset are out of market; they need to remember the brand, not decide today.
5. **Awareness levels** (Schwartz) are already covered in website-strategy stage 3, with the stated default "For most service websites, the primary visitor is Level 2-3" [B7]. Market sophistication is covered in the copywriting skill [B8].
6. **The customer's words are the raw material** (design-copy 03, citing Copyhackers' review mining) [B9]; website-strategy stage 3 "Language mirror" says the same [B7].

## Numbers
- 95 percent out of market in a quarter (B2B, Dawes), a heuristic; compute it from the category's interpurchase time [B6][B6b].
- CEP list: 5 to 10 candidates, one primary for the hero (opinion).
- Customer phrases: 20 to 50 verbatim (design-copy 03) [B9].

## Conflicting views
- Personas versus jobs: website-strategy stage 3 builds a named avatar with a 2am thought; JTBD and CEP work describe situations, not people. Not a real conflict (opinion): the avatar answers "who", the moment answers "when and why now"; the brief needs both in one line.
