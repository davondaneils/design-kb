# Research: Page copy structure

Status key: **C** consensus, **R** research-backed (single study or dataset), **P** practitioner opinion, **X** contested.

## Key findings

| # | Claim | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Users often leave a page within 10 to 20 seconds; pages that hold a visitor about 30 seconds tend to keep them for minutes. The value proposition must be clear in the first 10 seconds. | Nielsen, "How Long Do Users Stay on Web Pages?", NN/g, 2011 (Microsoft Research data, 205,873 pages, 2B+ dwell times) | R |
| 2 | Homepages must communicate who you are and what you do. Unless the name says it, include a tagline that says what you do. "Welcome to [Company]" explains nothing. Avoid "Click here", "Explore", "Learn more" labels. | Wang, "Homepage Design: 5 Fundamental Principles", NN/g, 2024 | C |
| 3 | Show specific examples of offerings rather than generic categories. | Same | C |
| 4 | Header litmus test: "If the visitor reads only this text on your page, will they know exactly what you sell?" Avoid slogans like "Supercharge your collaboration!" | Julian Shapiro, Landing Pages guide, julian.com | P |
| 5 | Subheader is one or two sentences: sentence 1 says what the product is, sentence 2 says how the header's claim is possible. | Shapiro | P |
| 6 | Address the single biggest buying objection in the header; others go in feature blocks. Survey customers: "What almost stopped you from buying?" | Shapiro | P |
| 7 | Default section order: navbar, hero, social proof, CTA, features and objections (3 to 6 features, each header + paragraph + image), repeat CTA, footer. Detouring from the template confuses visitors. | Shapiro | P |
| 8 | CTA is the natural next step from the header's claim ("Find food", "Start learning"). | Shapiro | P |
| 9 | Message layers in order: Clarity (what is it), Relevance (is it for me), Value (do I want it), Differentiation (why you). Fix the earliest failed layer, retest, then move on. One hypothesis and one revision per failed layer. | Wynter (Peep Laja's B2B message testing company), "B2B Message Layers Framework" | P (no data published) |
| 10 | Three tests for every line: Can I visualize it? Can I falsify it? Can nobody else say it? Examples: "32GB storage" to "1,000 songs in your pocket"; "Get fit again" to "Couch to 5K". | Harry Dry, Marketing Examples (@harrydry on X), via Demand Curve lesson and summaries | P (widely cited) |
| 11 | "Never write an ad your competitor can sign." | Jim Durfee, quoted by Harry Dry | P |
| 12 | Value proposition formula: "My product is the one that ___." Strong examples name audience, outcome, and a number ("saving you up to 40% of your workweek"). | Copyhackers (Joanna Wiebe), 2022 | P |
| 13 | "Powered by AI" is not a value proposition: describe the user goal (what), not the technology (how). | Samsonov, NN/g, 2025 | C |
| 14 | Designjoy hero: "Design subscriptions for everyone" + "Pause or cancel anytime." Category plus the main objection in 7 words. An earlier version, "design agency with a twist", was criticized by Indie Hackers commenters as unclear; one proposed "Unlimited design subscriptions to scale your business." | designjoy.co (fetched 2026-10); Indie Hackers launch thread | P (case example) |
| 15 | Concise text improved measured usability 58%, scannable layout 47%, objective (non-promotional) language 27%, all three combined 124%. | Nielsen, "How Users Read on the Web", NN/g, 1997 | R (old, still cited) |
| 16 | Copy at grade 5 to 7 converted at 11.1%, 56% higher than grade 8 to 9 (7.1%) and about double professional level (5.3%). Difficult words are the factor most linked to lower conversion. | Unbounce Conversion Benchmark Report 2024 (about 41,000 pages, 57M conversions), via DMN summary | R (correlation, not causation) |
| 17 | First-person CTA ("Start my free trial") beat second person ("your") by about 90% in one ContentVerve test. | Michael Aagaard / ContentVerve, widely re-cited | X (single test, rarely replicated) |
| 18 | Button copy tests are context dependent: "Submit" beat "Become a maven" and "I want in" in one WiderFunnel test; a supporting line above the button ("Join 110,023 Geek + Gamers Just Like YOU!") lifted conversion 14.1%. | Content Marketing Institute summary of WiderFunnel tests | R (unreported samples) |
| 19 | Picking between two builds of the Stand Firm quiz results page section by section, the client cut the sticky contents rail ("get rid of the section pills") and the "Sound like you?" confirmation taps ("get rid of the selection pills here"), kept the blind spot section ending on his own quiz answer about the cost ("I like the quote pull at the end of the Fable version"), and changed the close's "tomorrow" to "right now". 18 of 24 rows went to one build, 4 to the other, 1 mixed, 1 cut; the story order was not changed. | Davon's notes on the side-by-side board, 2026-10-29 (private artifact https://claude.ai/artifact/39fncsFLujdtSGXAih6ChW) | P (one client, one page) |
| 20 | Attention ratio is "the ratio of links on a landing page to the number of campaign conversion goals"; "in an optimized campaign, your attention ratio should be 1:1", with "only one call to action". | Unbounce conversion glossary, accessed 2026-10-29 | P |
| 21 | Reviewing the merged Stand Firm results page, the client cut the "Because you said" receipt (his answers quoted back with the type each counted for, the 3-1-0-0 tally, the fields "your report is built from"): "it doesnt add anything he needs or wants to see its just explanation." The quiz research had recommended echoing 2 to 3 answers verbatim but found no A/B data on it (kb/research/quiz/research_notes/Quiz funnel question design/data_and_personalisation.md, section 4). | Davon in the Stand Firm thread, 2026-10-29 21:37 UTC | P (one client, one page) |
| 22 | Same review: the client cut the secondary type everywhere on the results page ("With a Builder streak" under the reveal, and a fourth strength labeled "From your Builder streak"): "get rid of that so it just focuses on the one. its more clean this way." | Davon in the Stand Firm thread, 2026-10-29 21:44 UTC | P (one client, one page) |

## Conflicting views
- Clever vs clear: practitioners split. NN/g data and the Unbounce correlation favor clear. Brand studios use clever lines, but almost always with a plain subheadline doing the explaining. Rule adopted: clever is allowed only when the subheadline alone passes the "only this text" test.
- First-person CTAs: treat as a test hypothesis, not a rule.
