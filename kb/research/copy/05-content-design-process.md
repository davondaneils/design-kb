# Research: Content design process

Status key: **C** consensus, **R** research-backed, **P** practitioner opinion, **X** contested.

## Key findings

| # | Claim | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Average translation expansion from English, by English length: up to 10 chars 200 to 300%; 11 to 20: 180 to 200%; 21 to 30: 160 to 180%; 31 to 50: 140 to 160%; 51 to 70: 151 to 170%; over 70: 130%. Short strings expand most and sit in the tightest UI (labels, tabs, buttons). | IBM figures via W3C Internationalization, "Text size in translation" | R |
| 2 | Test the app without onboarding first; add it only if users struggle and a prototype measurably helps. | NN/g 2020 | C |
| 3 | Message testing per layer with consistent rating scales plus open-ended follow-up; fix the earliest failed layer first. | Wynter | P |
| 4 | Review mining builds the vocabulary list before drafting. | Copyhackers | P |
| 5 | A combined concise, scannable and objective rewrite improved usability 124%. | NN/g 1997 | R |
| 6 | Use the words users search for (check search terms). | GOV.UK clear language | C |
| 7 | Brett (Designjoy) on X, 2022: "If you're going to copy my business, which I actively encourage, at least write your own copy and design your own website." And: "Every design subscription agency looks exactly like Designjoy. Same design, same copy, same pricing." Copy as a differentiator, not a template. | x.com/BrettFromDJ/status/1513608707904458755 and /1724862055151493332 | P |
| 8 | Harry Dry's Marketing Examples is a curated, short-form library of copy and landing page teardowns ("How can I do the ad without any adjectives?", "Four ways to write a CTA"). A reference library, not a rulebook. | marketingexamples.com | P |
