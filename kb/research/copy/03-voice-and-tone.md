# Research: Voice and tone

Status key: **C** consensus, **R** research-backed, **P** practitioner opinion, **X** contested.

## Key findings

| # | Claim | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Four tone dimensions: formal vs casual, serious vs funny, respectful vs irreverent, matter-of-fact vs enthusiastic. Tone changes measurably shifted perceived friendliness and formality (50 US respondents, p < 0.05, differences of about 0.5 to 1 point on 5-point scales). | Moran, "The Four Dimensions of Tone of Voice", NN/g, 2016, reviewed 2023 | R |
| 2 | "You have the same voice all the time, but your tone changes." Attributes with boundaries: "weird but not inappropriate, smart but not snobbish". "It's always more important to be clear than entertaining." "Forced humor can be worse than none at all. If you're unsure, keep a straight face." Tone follows the reader's state of mind. | Mailchimp Content Style Guide, Voice and Tone | C (the canonical industry model) |
| 3 | Voice chart: voice attributes as columns; rows for Concepts, Vocabulary, Verbosity, Grammar, Punctuation, Capitalization. Each cell is a decision rule. | Podmajersky, *Strategic Writing for UX*, O'Reilly 2019; VTEX implementation | C |
| 4 | Use contractions (it's, you'll) to project friendliness. | Microsoft Writing Style Guide | C (for Microsoft) |
| 5 | Avoid negative contractions (can't, don't); write "cannot". Avoid should've, would've. | GOV.UK A to Z and clear language guidance | C (for GOV.UK) |
| 6 | Sentence case for headings and UI ("When in doubt, don't capitalize"). | Microsoft; GOV.UK | C |
| 7 | Voice of customer: mine reviews (including competitors') for six insight types: life problems, problems with current solutions, motivations, desires, favored features, anxieties. Track frequency and exact phrasing. Search pattern: site:amazon.com inurl:"product-reviews" "tired of" keyword. | Copyhackers, "Rapid-fire review mining" | P |
| 8 | Requirement wording: "must" for legal requirements, "need" for administrative steps, "can" for optional; avoid "may be able to". | GOV.UK clear language | C |
| 9 | Mentioning AI can trigger fear and reduce purchase intent; frame AI around the user's goal (Grammarly frames it as a writing partner). | NN/g 2025, citing a 2024 study | R (secondary) |

## Conflicting views
- Contractions: Microsoft yes, GOV.UK no (negative ones). Resolution adopted (opinion): positive contractions yes in casual voices; avoid negative contractions in instructions, warnings, legal text and anything a scanning eye could misread ("can't" vs "can"), especially for low-literacy audiences and in text that will be translated.
- Exclamation marks: no primary source sets a number. House default (opinion): at most one per screen, never in errors.
