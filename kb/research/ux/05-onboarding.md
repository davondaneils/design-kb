# Research: Onboarding

## Key findings

| # | Claim | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deck-of-cards tutorials (70 users, 4 iOS apps): no significant gain in task success (91% read vs 94% skipped) or time; readers rated tasks harder (SEQ 4.92 vs 5.49, significant). Many users skip tutorials. Prefer making the UI easy and contextual help. | NN/g [1] | R |
| 2 | Apple: onboarding should be fast, fun and optional; teach through interactivity; prefer context-specific tips over one flow; if a tutorial is skipped, do not show it again but keep it findable; postpone nonessential setup; provide defaults; ask ratings or purchases only after engagement; keep licensing out of onboarding. | Apple HIG Onboarding [2] | S (platform) |
| 3 | Apple: request permission only when needed, ideally when the person uses the feature; avoid launch-time requests unless the app cannot function without it; purpose strings are one specific sentence. A custom screen before the system alert is allowed, but no incentives, fake alerts, or images of the alert. | Apple HIG Privacy [3] | S (App Review enforced) |
| 4 | Empty states teach better than forced tutorials; offer demo data to explore safely. | NN/g [4] | C |
| 5 | 18% abandon checkout because account creation was required; 42% of sites do not defer account creation to the confirmation step. | Baymard [5][6] | R |
| 6 | Activation framing: define a single activation event that predicts retention and design onboarding to reach it; measure time to value. | Practitioner consensus (Samuel Hulick, userOnboard; product-led growth writers, widely shared on X) | P |
